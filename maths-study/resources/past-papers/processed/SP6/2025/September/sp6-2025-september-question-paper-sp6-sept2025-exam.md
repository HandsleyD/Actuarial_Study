2     (i)       Describe how a property total return swap works.                             [2]

      An analyst is estimating the value to a holder of a property index note at the start of a
      calendar year. The property index note has the following characteristics:

      Principal amount: $500,000.

      Maturity: 4 years.

      Coupon payments: It pays an annual coupon at the end of a calendar year based on
      the performance of a commercial property index over that year.

      Estimated commercial property index performance:

            Year 1: +4%
            Year 2: +2%
            Year 3: −3%
            Year 4: +5%.

      Estimated interest rates: The annually compounded spot interest rates for
      discounting are as follows:

            Year 1: 2%
            Year 2: 2.5%
            Year 3: 3%
            Year 4: 3.5%.

      Payout structure:

            The annual coupon payment (to or from the holder of the note up to and including
             maturity) is 1.5% of the principal amount multiplied by the index performance for
             that year.
            At maturity, the final capital payment to the buyer is the sum of the principal
             amount and an additional amount equal to the arithmetic sum index performance
             over the 4-year period, applied as a percentage of the principal.

      (ii)      Calculate, showing all workings, the analyst’s estimate of the present value of
                the coupon payments.                                                         [5]

      (iii)     Calculate, showing all workings, the analyst’s estimate of the present value of
                the property index note.                                                     [2]

      A hedge fund has a large portfolio of commercial property and faces large liquidity
      and macroeconomic risks in relation to this portfolio.

      (iv)      Discuss how the hedge fund can use derivatives to manage these risks.     [6]

      (ii)    Outline why an investor may choose to use a government bond futures

      A hedge fund held the view that the UK yield curve would steepen between the 2-year
      and the 5-year tenor.

      To reflect this view, the hedge fund bought £50 million notional of a 2-year Sterling
      Overnight Index Average (SONIA) swap at 3.1% and sold £25 million notional of a
      5-year SONIA swap at 3.4%. The swaps pay annual coupons. After 18 months, the
      SONIA yield curve with continuous compounding is as follows:

       Term    0.5   1.0   1.5   2.0   2.5   3.0   3.5
