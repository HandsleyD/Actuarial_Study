1    InsureCo is a specialist UK insurer that invests in credit assets with a maturity greater
     than 10 years. InsureCo uses a Value at Risk (VaR) model to calculate regulatory
     capital and this model is recalibrated each quarter. The VaR model allows credit risk
     drivers to be modelled appropriately for illiquid asset investments where this is
     possible, otherwise approximations are used. The VaR model is used to calculate
     regulatory capital at a fund level and at an overall level.

     (i)      Outline the key drivers of credit risk.                                       [2]

     (ii)     Explain the difficulty of calibrating a VaR model for a diversified credit
              portfolio that includes liquid and illiquid credit assets.                    [9]

     (iii)    Explain the principles of good corporate governance.                          [3]

     InsureCo’s internal asset manager is reviewing the governance related to investment
     decisions made for the credit portfolio. Due to a recent increase in illiquid assets held
     by insurers, the regulator has expressed concerns about the ability of insurers to assess
     and manage risks associated with investment in these assets.

     (iv)     Discuss the considerations for InsureCo’s illiquid investment framework with
              respect to:

              (a)      risk appetite.

              (b)      asset allocation limits and process.

              (c)      risk modelling capabilities.

2    A credit investor has a positive credit view on a sub-investment grade supermarket
     chain and would like to invest in some of the issuer’s debt. The supermarket chain has
     issued the following four types of debt: senior unsecured bond (high yield),
     syndicated loan, convertible bond and private credit (direct loan).

     (i)      Compare the four asset types in terms of payment profile, credit quality,
              security, liquidity and other relevant factors.                              [10]

     (ii)     For each of the following economic regimes, discuss how the four asset types
              may perform relative to each other. You should assume a starting position of
              moderate growth and moderate interest rates:

                   moderate growth (moderate growth, moderate interest rates)
                   recession (weak growth, low interest rates)
                   stagflation (weak growth, high interest rates)
                   strong growth (strong growth, rising interest rates).

3    A developed economy has been experiencing a period of weak economic growth and
     high inflation rates for some time. The government has been using an expansionary
     policy to stimulate the economy. However, the level of sovereign debt has now
     reached an unsustainably high level. As a result, investor confidence has fallen
     significantly and domestic market volatility has increased.

     A High Net Worth (HNW) individual’s equity portfolio is invested in passive funds.
     They have been advised to transfer their domestic assets to actively managed funds to
     take advantage of current market opportunities.

     (i)      Discuss the general conditions that need to be satisfied for actively managed
              funds to outperform passive funds.                                           [5]

     (ii)     Assess if the current market environment is likely to be one in which an active
              equity manager could outperform passive funds.                               [5]

     Previous poor active manager performance has caused the HNW individual to be
     biased against active management. A trusted friend who works in the vintage arts
     market has proposed that the individual moves 50% of their equity portfolio into a
     number of high value artworks until market volatility decreases.

     (iii)    Describe the advantages and disadvantages of this proposed change to the
              investment strategy.                                                     [10]
