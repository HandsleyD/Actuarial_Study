1    Recent projections for a developed country show an ageing population and a
     significant reduction in the ratio of workers to retirees in the country by 2050. As a
     result, the Department of Pensions is considering the introduction of an Automatic
     Enrolment (AE) pension scheme for all employed citizens who do not have employer
     pension schemes available to them. Contributions to the proposed AE scheme would
     be 6% by the employee, 6% by the employer and 2% by the state (as a proportion of
     the employee’s earnings).

     The Department of Pensions is unsure if it would be appropriate for young people to
     contribute to the proposed AE scheme where they do not yet own their own home. To
     help the department to make this decision, you have been asked to compare the
     projected benefits from the proposed AE scheme to the projected benefits from saving
     for a deposit on a home.

     You should make the following assumptions for the calculations in the rest of this
     question:

          The projections should be for a couple both aged 25 and both currently earning
           £42,000 per year (after tax).
          The AE scheme is invested in a lifestyle strategy: 80% in equities and 20% in
           bonds for the period from age 25 to age 55, and then over 10 years it linearly
           moves to 100% in bonds at age 65.
          AE scheme investment charges are 0.5% p.a. of the fund value.
          At age 65 the AE scheme assets are converted into an inflation-linked annuity
           with a real yield of 1%.
          Equity returns have an equity risk premium of 4% p.a. relative to bond returns.
          Bond returns are 1% p.a. above inflation.
          There is no tax relief on AE scheme contributions and both the AE scheme and
           the home are exempt from income and capital gains taxes.

     (i)      Estimate the following if the couple entered into the AE scheme and
              contributed until retirement:

              (a)     the couple’s combined pension fund at age 65 in real terms

              (b)     the pension that the combined fund would produce in real terms
                      assuming the pension is paid for 30 years after retirement at age 65.
                                                                                              [9]

     You are now asked to compare the relative benefit to the couple over the first
     10 years, of contributing to the AE scheme compared to saving for a deposit on a
     home.

     You should assume the following, in addition to the assumptions above:

           The couple’s savings will be placed into a bank account where cash deposits earn
            interest in line with inflation.
           The home will be purchased at a price of £250,000 in real terms in 10 years’ time.
           The couple will purchase the home using the total funds saved in the bank account
            plus a mortgage to fund the balance.
           The mortgage will have a duration of 30 years.
           Mortgage repayments (interest and capital) are equal to the cost of renting a home.
           Residential property prices increase in line with equity returns less 1% p.a.
           Cash deposits earn interest in line with inflation.
           Ignore any payments made into the AE scheme after 10 years.
           The employer and state contributions are only available when saving in the AE
            scheme and not if saving in a bank account.

     (ii)      Estimate the expected values, at age 65, of contributing to the AE scheme,
               compared to saving for a deposit on a home and comment on the difference
               between the two.                                                           [9]

     (iii)     Comment, with reasons, on whether the economic and other assumptions
               provided may or may not be valid.                                             [6]

     (iv)      Discuss the implications of your projections for the state’s design of the AE
               scheme in terms of whether it would meet the aim of managing the likely
               future demographic change.                                                    [6]

2    The government of a developing nation manages a $10 billion Defined Benefit (DB)
     pension scheme for its civil servants, with about one-third of its assets and liabilities
     relating to pensioners. As part of a reform program, the government will switch
     civil servants’ pensions into Defined Contribution (DC) scheme for future service,
     with existing DB benefits preserved. Annual DC contributions are expected to be
     $500 million and DC members will draw down their funds during retirement in the
     scheme.

     The government wishes to outsource investment management for the combined DB
     and DC scheme to a fiduciary manager following the switch. The government will
     define the high-level asset allocation and oversee the pension scheme, with portfolio
     construction, manager selection and reporting outsourced. You are working in the
     institutional sales team of a fiduciary manager and are helping write the response to
     the invitation to tender.

     Comment on:

     (i)      the differences between the DB and DC assets in such a scheme.                 [6]

     (ii)     the key considerations for the fiduciary manager when responding to the
              invitation to tender.                                                         [8]

     [Hint: Consider the nature, term and size of the liabilities, asset allocation differences
     and liquidity requirements.]
                                                                                      [Total 14]


3    The head of responsible investment at a large insurer is looking at ways to increase
     Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) integration in the insurer’s investment
     process. Rather than set up their own security-level analytics for their listed equity
     and bond investments, they are considering using external vendors of ESG ratings.
     The aim is to use ESG ratings as part of the investment decision-making process when
     determining the attractiveness of individual equities and bonds.

     (i)      Explain why it may be beneficial to use an external vendor of ESG ratings as
              an input into the investment process, rather than the insurer creating its own
              security-level analytics.                                                      [5]

     (ii)     List ESG factors that could be considered in the calculation of ESG scores
              and ratings.                                                               [3]

     The head of equities is concerned that ESG scores for the same company can diverge
     significantly across different external vendors and that some vendors have several
     ratings methodologies. They have suggested creating internal ESG scores based on
     ratings from two or three leading vendors.

     (iii)    Explain, giving examples, why ESG scores may differ significantly across
              different data providers.                                                [4]

     (iv)     Discuss some of the complexities with creating internal ESG scores in the
              manner suggested.                                                          [6]
                                                                                  [Total 18]

4    The head of fixed income investments of a life insurer wants to expand the fixed
     income asset classes that the insurer can invest in to include directly held private
     loans. Each direct loan investment will need to be approved by the investment
     committee prior to investment. To assist with the approval process the investment
     committee has asked the investment team to design a two-page investment due
     diligence document that will set out key details for each proposed loan investment.

     Set out the items to be included in the due diligence document.                     [10]


5    A developed country has been experiencing significant economic and political
     instability. The country’s largest pension scheme is currently invested only in
     domestic bonds and equities. In light of the current instability, the scheme’s
     investment advisor has proposed making a 10% allocation to a global macro hedge
     fund and has recommended two managers with strong recent performance. Further
     due diligence will need to be carried out before a decision is made on which to invest
     with.

     (i)      Describe how the performance of a hedge fund could be assessed, suggesting
              suitable metrics.                                                        [5]

     (ii)     Discuss factors that could lead to strong performance by a hedge fund
              manager, which should be investigated as part of the investment due diligence
              process.                                                                   [9]

     Following further analysis, the investment advisor has proposed a further proportion
     of the scheme’s assets be allocated to improve risk-adjusted returns. In particular they
     recommend commodities and insurance-linked securities.

     (iii)    Discuss the investment advisor’s recommendation, giving the pros and cons
              of:

              (a)    commodities.                                                           [7]

              (b)    insurance-linked securities.                                         [7]
