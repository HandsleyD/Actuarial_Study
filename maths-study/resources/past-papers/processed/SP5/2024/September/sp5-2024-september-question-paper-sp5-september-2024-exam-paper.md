1     A fund management group manages a number of funds including Fund A, which
      invests in the S&P 500 and Fund B, which invests in the MSCI US mid cap index.

      An investor has said that in 1 month’s time they wish to switch their holding in Fund
      A into Fund B. The fund manager has indicated the terms that would apply if the
      switch were made immediately. The fund manager has also offered to guarantee those
      terms until the investor’s proposed switching date in 1 month’s time.

      The fund manager has decided to use hedging to ensure that the terms of the switch
      remain the same.

      (i)     Describe two types of hedging that are relevant to this objective.            [2]

      (ii)    Describe how the fund manager may use the two types of hedging you
              identified in part (i) to achieve their required goal.                        [2]

      (iii)   Discuss the issues the fund manager may encounter if they did use hedging in
              the situation described above.                                             [2]

      (iv)    State what is meant by an optimal hedge ratio.                                [1]

      The fund manager has the following information:

                                                          Standard deviation
               Fund A return                                    0.13
               Fund B return                                    0.15
               S&P 500 future price                             0.15
               MSCI US mid cap future price                     0.17

      In both cases the correlation coefficient between the future and the fund price is 0.83.

      (v)     Calculate the hedge ratio for the two transactions described in part (ii).    [4]

      (vi)    Describe the transactions that the fund management group would undertake in
              order to enter into these hedging arrangements.                          [1]

      (vii)   Describe another approach the fund manager could use to achieve the required
              result.                                                                   [2]

2     A small defined benefit pension scheme is closed to new entrants and there is no
      further benefit accrual for members. An outline of the scheme’s asset and liability
      structure is shown below. The assets of the scheme are invested in index-tracking
      pooled funds.

                                                                          Duration
      Liability value (based on government bond yields)      $100 million 15 years
      Proportion of liabilities sensitive to inflation       60%          –
      Annual benefit payments                                $2 million   –
      Assets
         Global equities                                     $45 million    –
         Fixed interest government bonds                     $25 million    12 years
         Inflation-linked government bonds                   $25 million    20 years
         Short-dated bond/cash fund                          $5 million     0 years

      The trustees of the scheme are responsible for its investment strategy and have agreed
      with the sponsoring employer to change the scheme’s asset portfolio. The trustees’
      and the employer’s objective is to minimise the risk that a deficit will materialise in
      the future.

      (i)     Explain why the trustees and the sponsoring employer would adopt this
              objective.                                                                    [2]

      (ii)    Describe the investment risks the scheme currently faces, with particular
              regard to the objective stated above.                                         [4]

      The trustees wish to create an investment portfolio consistent with the objective stated
      above. They do not wish to introduce any new funds, but rather, to alter the allocation
      to the existing ones.

      (iii)   Determine a suitable portfolio. You should calculate the value of the scheme’s
              assets to be held in each fund.                                            [6]

      (iv)    Describe the disadvantages of using inflation-linked government bonds in a
              portfolio to protect against unexpected inflation.                         [3]

3     An investor adopts an investment strategy with 60% in equities and 40% in fixed
      income. The investor has appointed a fund manager to invest the assets in a
      segregated portfolio. The fund manager will be expected to manage the equities and
      bonds actively versus certain equity or bond indices.

      The investor is considering two approaches:

            To invest their assets without subsequent adjustments to the asset allocation
            To instruct the fund manager to rebalance to the 60%/40% allocation at the end of
             each year.

      Historic data for the benchmark indices and the selected fund manager’s annual
      performance against the indices are as follows:

                                Index returns       Fund manager returns
                             Equities    Bonds       Equities   Bonds
                             (% p.a.) (% p.a.)       (% p.a.)  (% p.a.)
                     2018      7.0        −1.0         6.0        0.0

                     2020      6.0        7.0          4.0        4.0
                     2021     −10.0       0.0         −5.0        2.0
                     2022     15.0        −6.0        10.0       −4.0
                     2023      1.5        −8.0         1.0       −6.0

      (i)       Calculate the benchmark value in $ at the end of 2023 assuming an investment
