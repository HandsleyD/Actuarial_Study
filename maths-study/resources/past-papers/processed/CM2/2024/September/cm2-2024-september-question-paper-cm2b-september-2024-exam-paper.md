2    The ‘Q2 data’ worksheet gives details of the holdings in a portfolio of four different
     options, A, B, C, D, and two different shares, E, F. The option prices have been
     calculated using the Black–Scholes formula. The risk-free force of interest is 6% p.a.

     (i)     Calculate the missing values X, Y and Z from the data tables in the ‘Q2 data’
             worksheet.                                                                  [8]

     (ii)    Demonstrate that put-call parity holds in the portfolio.                      [3]

     (iii)   Calculate the total value of the portfolio.                                   [2]

     The portfolio holder decides to create a delta hedged portfolio consisting only of
     Share E, Option A and Option C. They will sell their existing holdings in Option B,
     Option D and Share F and reinvest the proceeds in Option A and Option C. They are
     not able to change their current holding of Share E.

     (iv)    Determine the number of units of Option A and Option C required to create a
             delta hedged portfolio.                                                   [9]

     Immediately after the portfolio is constructed, Share E starts paying dividends
     continuously at a rate of 8% p.a.

     (v)     Calculate the change in the value of the portfolio, assuming there is no change
             in the price of Share E.                                                      [6]

4    A share on Company A currently trades at $140. An investor wishes to add
     Company A to their portfolio but does not have sufficient money to buy the stock
     directly. The investor is therefore considering purchasing a European call option on
     the stock with a term of 10 years and a strike price of $140. The risk-free force of
     interest is 5% p.a. No dividends are payable during the life of the option.

     The volatility of the share is 32%.

     (i)       Calculate the value of the call option using the Black–Scholes option pricing
               model.                                                                      [3]

     The investor is also considering purchasing a 10-year forward contract as an
     alternative to the call option.

     (ii)      Calculate the price of the forward contract.                                    [1]

     An at-the-money European put option on Company A’s stock is being offered
     exclusively to the investor for $18 per option.

     (iii)     (a)     Demonstrate that this offer represents an arbitrage opportunity.        [1]

               (b)     Explain how the investor could profit from this arbitrage opportunity.
                                                                                            [2]

     The following share price projection model has been proposed to model the call
     option:

                                     St = S0 exp[(r − ½σ2)t + σZt]

     where

           S0 = $140
           r = 5.5%
           σ = 30%
           Term = 10 years
           Zt is a standard Brownian motion.

     An economic scenario generator has produced 100 simulations of a discrete time
     series {Zt, t ≥ 0}. The simulations can be found in the ‘Q4 data’ worksheet.

     (iv)      Calculate the price of the call option using this simulation model.             [8]

     (v)       Explain why your answer to part (iv) is different to your answer to part (i).
