2    A company decides to give each of its employees a new benefit. Each employee will
     receive 1,000 non-dividend paying shares in the company in 1 year’s time, provided
     the share price has increased from its current level of $1.00 to at least $1.50.

     You may assume the following parameters:

           Risk-free interest rate: 4% per year continuously compounded
           Stock price volatility: 30% per year.

     (i)       (a)    Explain, by considering the components of the Black–Scholes formula,
                      how this formula can be used to value the benefit to each employee.

               (b)    Calculate the value of the benefit to each employee.
                                                                                            [9]

     The company now wishes to limit the gain to each employee to a maximum of
     $2,000.

     (ii)      (a)    Set out how your answer to part (i)(a) may differ, if this limit is
                      introduced.

               (b)    Calculate the value to the employee of this revised benefit.
                                                                                            [6]

     An employee believes the original benefit, without the upper limit, is worth $300.
     They have determined this by assuming that a 30% chance of the share price being at
     least $1.50 in a year’s time, and applying this to the current share price.

     (iii)     Comment on the employee’s approach.                                          [3]

     Now, assume that a market exists in which the benefit can be traded, by
     market-makers, as an investment contract.

     (iv)     Discuss the implications, in such a market, arising from the employee’s
              valuation of the benefit.                                                   [3]

3    An insurance company holds the following credit-risky assets with 2 years to
     maturity:

                                 Number of assets held
              Credit rating                                     Description
                                    at time t = 0
                   A                    1,000                 Highest rating
                   B                    1,000              Second highest rating
                   C                    1,000               Third highest rating
                   D                      0                       Default

     The transitions that can occur between credit ratings in any year are set out in the
     following table:

                                                         Year 1      Year 2
                                                          (%)         (%)
                Exactly one rating downgrade               20          15
                Exactly two rating downgrade               10           8
                Exactly three rating downgrade              4           2

     Downgrades that would take an asset lower than credit rating D should be ignored.

     There can be at most one transition to a different rating in a single year. There are no
     upgrades. There are no other ways that an asset can leave or enter the insurer’s credit
     portfolio.

     (i)      Calculate, for each year, the transition matrix that shows the expected number
              of assets moving between credit states.                                     [8]

     (ii)     Construct, for each year, the 1-year transition rate matrix using your answer
              from part (i).                                                                [6]

     (iii)    Calculate, for each credit rating at time t = 0, the probability that a default
              would have occurred by time t = 2.                                              [8]

     A 2-year zero-coupon risk-free bond P(0,2) has the following features at time t = 0:

                                    Redemption     Maturity
                                      $1,000          2

     A credit-risky bond has the following features at time t = 0:

             Redemption       Maturity   Credit rating   Recovery rate on default
               $1,000            2            A                   75%

     The risk-free force of interest is 5% p.a.

     (iv)     Calculate the implied credit spread (i.e. the return above the risk-free rate) for
              the credit-risky bond.                                                          [7]

     (v)      Explain, without carrying out any further calculations, how the credit spread
              may change if the probability of default increased.                          [3]
