1      About our Campsite Insurance policy

What does BRP’s Campsite Insurance cover?

Our Campsite Insurance policy is specifically designed to protect against the unique risks
posed to campsites and glamping sites.

Unlike comparable policies offered by other insurers, which only provide public liability
cover as standard, our policy provides:

   Public liability cover
    Should a guest injure themselves at your campsite and seek compensation, public liability
    insurance can cover the legal costs arising from defending a claim and damages you may
    need to pay to the injured party.

   Business interruption cover
    Should unforeseen circumstances (for example flooding or major storm) prevent your
    campsite from operating as usual, you may lose income. Business interruption cover is
    designed to help cover this loss until you are operational again.

   Property damage cover
    Should damage occur to your campsite from an incident such as a fire, flood or burglary,
    this is covered under property damage cover. This includes harm to site amenities and
    structures.

This means that you can be sure that you have an excellent level of protection with BRP
Campsite Insurance.

3      Note issued by the Insurance Oversight Board

N38: Insurers’ exposure to climate change risk (non-life)

The impact of global warming is already being felt around the world, both on natural and
human systems. The average global temperature has risen by 1°C since the pre-industrial era,
and is predicted to continue rising. The warming from emissions caused by human activity is
likely to lead to further long-term changes including an increase in the frequency and severity
of natural catastrophes and climate-related extreme events. Examples of climate-related
hazards:

   Windstorms
    Meteorological extra-tropical cyclones, a type of low-pressure cyclonic system in middle
    and high latitudes. They are characterised by strong winds, heavy rain or snow.

   Wildfires
    Climatological disasters, the uncontrolled combustion or burning in wildland vegetation
    that spreads based on environmental conditions.

   Coastal floods
    Hydrological disasters that occur when seawater inundates low-lying coastal areas.

   River floods
    Hydrological disasters caused by a river overflowing its banks and inundating the
    surrounding area.

As a sector that offers financial protection against the consequences of such events, the
effects of climate change on the pricing of risks must be considered.

Many non-life insurers issue short-term contracts that allow for re-pricing annually. It has
been common practice in the industry not to include climate change risk in pricing
methodology and assume that it would be implicitly reflected at the point of re-price, based
on historical data. It is our view that modelling techniques based on the projection of past
data do not fully capture the emerging climate trends.

We urge all insurers to review their pricing models to ensure that they take into account
climate change risk.

Insurers should consider how they are assessing physical risk. Data on physical risk hazards
and vulnerability should be used along with granular information on the geo-spatial
characteristics of the exposure.

Glossary of terms

Physical risk: The risk of damage and financial losses caused by the physical impacts of
climate change.

Transition risk: The risk of financial losses related to the transition to a lower-carbon
economy.

Geo-spatial data: Location specific data.

4        BRP pricing approach

The aim of our pricing approach is to ensure that premium rates are set at an appropriate
level that reflect the risk to BRP. That is, the premium rates should adequately reflect the
likelihood and expected size of a claim.

The following philosophy underpins our pricing approach:

    The risk premium must be calculated using policyholder specific details to ensure we are
     correctly reflecting the risk.
    Best estimates should not have margins added so as to avoid unnecessary prudence.

Prices will be updated annually using generalised linear models. These will be reviewed by
the Pricing Committee. The Pricing Committee comprises the Head of Pricing, the
Commercial Director and the Chief Risk Officer.

The variables that have been determined to impact the claim costs and, therefore, used to
determine premiums are:

X1       Average nightly charge per guest
X2       Maximum occupancy
X3       Campsite size
X4       Number of employees
X5       On-site facilities
X6       Season length.

Notes:

1 Maximum occupancy indicates the maximum number of guests that can be onsite at any
  time. This will not necessarily be the maximum as specified on the campsite licence
  (issued by the local authority) but as determined by the campsite based on their business
  approach.

2 The campsite size is the total area of the campsite; this should include all buildings and
  fields even if not accessible to guests. It is measured in metres squared.

3 Three categories are used for on-site facilities: none, standard, extensive. The definitions
  of these categories are in the terms and conditions of the policy, and available to all IFAs.

4 Season length is the number of months the campsite is open to guests, rounded up to the
  nearest integer of months.
