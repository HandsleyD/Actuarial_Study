Introduction

The Examiners’ Report is written by the Chief Examiner with the aim of helping candidates, both
those who are sitting the examination for the first time and using past papers as a revision aid and
also those who have previously failed the subject.

The Examiners are charged by Council with examining the published syllabus. The Examiners
have access to the Core Reading, which is designed to interpret the syllabus, and will generally
base questions around it but are not required to examine the content of Core Reading specifically
or exclusively.

For numerical questions the Examiners’ preferred approach to the solution is reproduced in this
report; other valid approaches are given appropriate credit. For essay-style questions, particularly
the open-ended questions in the later subjects, the report may contain more points than the
Examiners will expect from a solution that scores full marks.

For some candidates, this may be their first attempt at answering an examination using open
books and online. The Examiners expect all candidates to have a good level of knowledge and
understanding of the topics and therefore candidates should not be overly dependent on open book
materials. In our experience, candidates that spend too long researching answers in their materials
will not be successful either because of time management issues or because they do not properly
answer the questions.

Many candidates rely on past exam papers and examiner reports. Great caution must be exercised
in doing so because each exam question is unique. As with all professional examinations, it is
insufficient to repeat points of principle, formula or other text book works. The examinations are
designed to test “higher order” thinking including candidates’ ability to apply their knowledge to
the facts presented in detail, synthesise and analyse their findings, and present conclusions or
advice. Successful candidates concentrate on answering the questions asked rather than repeating
their knowledge without application.

Candidates should note that from the April 2025 exam session, all examinations will
continue to be delivered virtually and will have online proctoring. Exams will be closed
book and closed web. The ability to refer to past examiner reports and past papers during
the exam is not permitted. Candidates attempting to do so will be in breach of the
Assessment Regulations and subject to inappropriate conduct investigations. Further
details of the new exams can be found on the IFOA website.


The report is written based on the legislative and regulatory context pertaining to the date that the
examination was set. Candidates should take into account the possibility that circumstances may
have changed if using these reports for revision.

A.     General comments on the aims of this subject and how it is marked


       1. Written communication [80 marks]

The assessment requires candidates to produce a written piece of communication that explains a
scenario typically faced by an actuary in their day-to-day work. This communication will be
aimed at a non-actuary, although the target audience’s level of financial knowledge and
understanding will vary from question to question.

The communication needs to be of a standard that would be acceptable as a first draft. It is
important that the recipient would both understand the communication and be satisfied with the
response. The marking schedules include details of the marks awarded including the necessary
content. To the extent that it makes the communication unclear or confusing for the audience,
marks may also be lost for including irrelevant content or details that candidates have specifically
been asked to exclude from their solution.

       2. Reflective questions [20 marks]

These are a set of questions designed to allow candidates to consider the approach that they took
in their communication, and justify certain decisions. For example, candidates may be asked what
information they felt was relevant for this audience, or which terms they specifically excluded
because they would constitute jargon.

Candidates are provided with some background reading a few days before the exam (the Scenario
Material) to allow them to familiarise themselves with the scenario without being under exam
conditions. Candidates are expected to read the information provided but are not required to do
any further reading or research around the scenario.

B.     Comments on candidate performance in this diet of the examination

This exam asked candidates to draft a paper for insurance intermediaries to explain some changes
to the pricing approach for the campsite insurance product. It is the job of these intermediaries to
sell insurance products such as campsite insurance to businesses. As such, it is reasonable to
assume that they have a good basic knowledge of insurance and the motivations that business will
have to buying products such as the one at the heart of this scenario. However, the question paper
does remind candidates that some intermediaries may be new to the industry, and we should not
assume specific knowledge about the terms and conditions of this product.

One of the key objectives in CP3 is to identify the level of competence of the audience, and in
particular the characteristics of the least sophisticated reader. Candidates should then aim their
paper at the least sophisticated reader to ensure that it can be understood by all. In this paper, it
was important to explain points of detail around the product, but general concepts of insurance,
and the benefits of having insurance in place are unnecessary. Where students struggled with this
paper, it was generally because they explained very basic concepts in too much detail and did not
give enough of an overview of the implications of the change.

C.     Pass Mark

The Pass Mark for this exam was 55
1692 presented themselves and 966 passed


A pass mark of 55 was judged to be the mark awarded to the minimally competent candidate. This
paper was judged to be particularly challenging because there were a lot of specific content points
that needed to be included in a student's paper to score full marks on parts (vii), (viii) and (ix) of
question 1. It was possible to score relatively poorly on these sections while still producing a
paper that allowed the audience in the scenario to grasp the key messages.

A student scoring 55 produced a paper that explained the key points in a way that the audience
would have grasped without providing significant unnecessary information that would have
detracted from the key messages. For question 2 students demonstrated that they understood and
could apply the key syllabus objectives.

It was noted that there were a surprising number of cases where candidates had correctly

Q1

To: All Intermediaries of BRP Limited

From: BRP Pricing Team

24 September 2023



                        Changes to Premium following Pricing Review

Introduction

This paper is addressed to Intermediaries of BRP Limited (“BRP) and has been produced by
the Pricing team. Following a recent request by the Insurance Oversight Board (IOB) we have
reviewed our pricing approach, with a particular focus on climate change risk, and made some
changes to the premium calculation.

This paper sets out the following:

     •   Why climate change is of concern to BRP
     •   The changes made to our pricing approach
     •   The impact of the changes on premiums



Climate Change Risk

The IOB recently issued guidance to all insurers which highlighted the need to consider
climate change within pricing. As extreme weather events, such as floods and wildfires are
becoming more common, the regulator wants to ensure that insurers are including the impact
of climate change within their premiums.

As widely reported within the press there has been an increase in the frequency and severity
of extreme weather events. If this trend continues, as is expected, more people and businesses
will be affected by weather events and there will be more insurance claims being made. For
insurers who provide insurance cover for weather events, it is likely that they will see both a
higher number of claims and larger claims in future.

At BRP, our campsite insurance policy offers policyholders insurance for, amongst other
things, extreme weather events. The business interruption cover applies when a campsite has
to close due to unforeseen circumstances, which could be weather related. We also provide
cover for damage to the campsite caused by weather events, under the material damage cover
clause.



Changes to pricing approach

We review our pricing approach annually to ensure that the premium charged for each policy
reflects the expected risk to BRP. The expected risk is based on both the likelihood of a claim
and the likely size of the claim. If a policyholder is more likely to claim then they are charged
a higher premium accordingly.

The IOB asked insurers to ensure that their pricing approach takes into account climate
change risk. So, in our most recent annual pricing review we also considered climate change
risk and whether our premiums currently allow for this.

While we are happy with our general approach to pricing, we have decided that our premium
calculation should better reflect the risk of flooding. To do this, we have added flood zone to
the calculation. There is no need for you to gather additional information from the
policyholder, the flood zone is determined from the campsite postcode which you already
provide. Postcodes are assigned to one of three flood zones by the Local Flood Authority,
depending on the probability of river or coastal flooding. By including the flood zone data in
our calculation, we feel this will better reflect the expected risk from each policy. Those
policyholders more at risk of flood will be charged a higher premium as they have a higher
chance of claiming.

Impact on premiums

The addition of flood zone data in our premium calculation is effective immediately for new
policies and at renewal for existing policies. As this is a change to the calculation,
policyholders will see a difference in the premium quoted following this change. The chart
below shows the premium for a typical policy over the last 4 years, along with the premium
this year for each of the three possible flood zones.

            CP3 ‑ Communications Practice ‑ Core Practices - September 2024 - Examiners’ Report




Policyholders in flood zone 3 will see their premium increase the most, those in flood zone 1
will see a fall in premium and those in flood zone 2 will see an increase similar to the annual
increase in previous years. Currently over 50% of policyholders are within flood zone 1, so
the change to our premium calculation will result in decreased premiums for a large number
of policyholders. For those in flood zone 3, around 18% of current policyholders, the typical
premium will increase by 19%.



Summary

The premiums BRP charges policyholders should reflect the expected risk of the policy.
Climate change is resulting in more frequent and severe weather events. The increased risk of
these weather events and climate change risk must be considered within premium
calculations. To better reflect the risk to BRP our premium calculation will now include flood
zone data. Most current policyholders will see a fall in their premium or a small increase in
line with previous annual increases. The premium for policyholders in flood zone 3 will
increase to reflect the increased risk of a claim. No other changes have been made to our
premium calculations.

If you would like any further information please do not hesitate to contact the Head of
Pricing.
