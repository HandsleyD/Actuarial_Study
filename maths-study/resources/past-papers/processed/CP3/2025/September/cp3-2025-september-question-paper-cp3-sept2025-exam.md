You are a member of the actuarial team that advises the trustees of the ABC Pension Scheme.

You have received the following email from your manager:

To: Sidney Green, Actuary, Top Consultants
From: Nishith Patel, Actuarial Manager, Top Consultants

Hi Sidney,

I have had an email from one of the trustees of the ABC Pension Scheme. A trustee has read
a newspaper article with negative comments about one of the options available for allowing
for the changes required from the recent Broke Judgement. They would like some more
details so that they can better understand how the two options affect members of the ABC
Pension Scheme.

Please can you draft a short paper for the next trustees meeting setting out:

   the basic details of the Broke Judgement.
   the two options available to the ABC Pension Scheme to allow for the judgement.
   how the two options may impact members.
   any other aspects that may be relevant to the trustees that they should consider before
    making their decision.

The decision on which option to go for is for the trustees to make – so you do not need to
provide a recommendation. Please remember that the trustees have varied backgrounds and
any examples you produce need to be easily understood by all of them. At the last trustees
meeting, the trustees were complimentary on how the graphs and tables we produced
illustrated the issues, so I suggest that you include at least two graphs or tables in your paper.

This paper will be added to the agenda for the next trustees meeting and I will talk the
trustees through the paper that you produce and answer any questions they may have at that
meeting.

Thanks,
Nishith

Additional notes on the Broke Judgement:

The newspaper article referred to in the email has been reproduced below:

‘Broke’ ruling: How your pension increases may be erratic

The latest ruling to affect pensioners is aptly named the ‘Broke’ ruling. Although the ruling
refers to the firm at the centre of the court case, Broke Wheel Suspensions, the impact on
pensioners may make them go broke!

The judgement requires pension schemes to provide men and women with identical salary
and company service history with the same pension from retirement age onwards and gives
pension schemes two options for complying.

The first option means that men and women always receive the pension that is higher for a
man or a woman and it provides them with nice regular increases. Just what you need as a
pensioner on a budget.

However, the second option, although it appears similar, allows pension schemes to delay
increasing the pensions to the higher of the male and female pension. Pension increases under
this option are more erratic. There are some ages where you may receive lower increases than
you would expect and then suddenly you may get a decent increase to catch-up. Uneven
pension increases are tricky when you are on a budget, as pensioners are. Will your energy
company be happy when you say you can’t pay them this year – but may be able to in a few
years? ‘No’.

If you are worried about how the ‘Broke Judgement’ may affect your retirement income, then
you may want to write to your pension scheme to find out what they are doing.
