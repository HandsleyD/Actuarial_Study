Introduction
Top Consultants is an actuarial consultancy that advises pension scheme trustees. The
trustees of the ABC Pension Scheme are advised by Top Consultants.

There has been a recent judgement (known as the ‘Broke Judgement’) in the High Court that
affects the pension schemes that Top Consultants advises.

You have been provided with the following information:

1. A summary of the Broke Judgement.

2. Some details from a student actuary employed by Top Consultants on how the Broke
   Judgement will affect a typical member of the ABC Pension Scheme. This includes a
   description of how pension increases are calculated and a table of figures that illustrate
   the impact of the judgement. (A typical member is broadly representative of the
   membership profile of the ABC Pension Scheme.)

3. Details of how the administration of pension schemes may be affected by the change.




CP3 S2025–2
1      A summary of the Broke Judgement
Date of judgement: 1 September 2025.

Background: Prior to the Broke Judgement, it would have been possible for members with
the same company service and pay to receive different pensions over time due to the different
pension increase rates that apply to different parts of their pension. Typically, males would
receive a higher pension in the earlier stages of retirement while females would receive a
higher pension in the later stages.

Ruling: Pensions in payment must be the same for males and females who have the same
company service and pay. The ruling only applies for pensioners who retire on or after the
date of the judgement, i.e. who retire on or after 1 September 2025.

Methods of compliance: Two options are available to each pension scheme for complying
with the ruling:

1. Pension in payment should be the higher of the male and female pension at all ages.
2. The scheme may offset payments made to males in excess of the female pension before
   switching to the female pension. The switch to the female pension should be made when
   the higher payment to males in earlier years is broadly offset by the higher payments that
   would have been due to females.

Time constraints: The industry regulator has confirmed that schemes have a maximum
of 1 year to make any changes needed.

Key details for this ‘typical’ member

   Total pension on retirement at age 60 is equal for males and females ($1,000.00 p.a.)
   Total pension = ‘Basic’ + ‘Additional’ pension
   Total pension is payable yearly in advance
   ‘Basic’ pension receives guaranteed increases of 2% p.a.
   ‘Additional’ pension receives guaranteed increases of 5% p.a.
   The split of the total pension at age 60 is different for males and females
   Males start with an ‘Additional’ pension of $245.00 p.a. at age 60
   All female pensions at age 60 comprise ‘Basic’ pension only
   At age 65, female total pension is split into ‘Basic’ and ‘Additional’ pensions. The
    ‘Additional’ pension at age 65 for females is $600.00 p.a.
   This results in different increases after retirement for men and women who start with the
    same pension.

The cross-over age (N) where the female total pension is higher than the male total pension
for the first time can be determined as the lowest integer N where the following condition is
met. The formula is only valid for N ≥ 65.

Bm × 1.02(N − 60) + Am × 1.05(N − 60) < (Bf × 1.02(65 − 60) − 600) × 1.02(N − 65) + 600 × 1.05(N − 65)

where:

   Bm = Male ‘Basic’ pension at age 60 = $755.00 p.a.
   Am = Male ‘Additional’ pension at age 60 = $245.00 p.a.
   Bf = Female ‘Basic’ pension at age 60 = $1,000.00 p.a.

[Note: The female ‘Additional’ pension that is split out from the female total pension at age

3      Details of how administration may be affected by the Broke
       Judgement
Top Consultants also provide administration services to the pension schemes that they advise.
A general solution (‘Broke Generalised Pension Increase (PI) Calculation’) has been created
so that the yearly pension increase calculations can cater for both options allowed under the
judgement.

The following information is relevant to how the administration team will account for the
‘Broke Generalised PI Calculation’:

   A new pension increase calculation has been added to cater for both Option 1 and
    Option 2.
   A new data variable ‘Broke Jud’ has been added to the administrative database.
   ‘Broke Jud’ will be used to determine the pension increases that should apply. The
    permitted values are ‘NA’, ‘OP1’ and ‘OP2’.
   All pensions already in payment at 31 August 2025 have had ‘NA’ added to their records,
    as they are not impacted by the Broke Judgement.
   New pensioners on or after 1 September 2025 will have ‘Broke Jud’ completed as part of
    their retirement routine.
   Where Option 1 is chosen by a scheme, affected members will have ‘OP1’ added to their
    records.
   Where Option 2 is chosen by a scheme, affected members will have ‘OP2’ added to their
    records.
   Individual schemes must apply a single option to all affected members. Where a mix of
    ‘OP1’ and ‘OP2’ exists for a single scheme then a warning message will appear on
    affected member records ‘Inconsistent Broke Jud entries’.
   Continuing administration costs of applying Option 1 and Option 2 are the same.

Full testing on the increases has been carried out in a Secure Remote Testing Environment
and the new calculations will be moved into the live phase in the next month.

The administrative services make a charge when members raise questions. In the past, very
few members have raised questions when they receive an annual pension increase as the
increases awarded are guaranteed. The existing standard letters sent out to pensioners are also
clear and meet the Regulator’s guidance on ‘clarity and simplicity’.

Top Consultants have drafted appropriate new letters to cover regular pension increases under
both options. If members start to raise questions because of the change in pension increases,
this will result in extra costs to the pension scheme. The student actuary has calculated that
the maximum additional costs if all affected members were to raise questions and require
further information from the administration team for the ABC Pension Scheme is expected to
be less than $9,875.32.
