You are an actuary working in the government’s actuary division of Country X. You have
received the following email from your manager.

From: Roberta.Lopez@govx.com
To: Robin.Jones@govx.com
Sent: 23 April 2025; 08.45
Subject: Concerns about the sustainability of our state pension arrangements

Dear Robin,

In a meeting at the Treasury on Monday, the Minister of Finance raised the topic of state
pension arrangements. They had read the article in the Daily News that I have already shared
with you, which highlights the challenges raised by our ageing population. There was general
concern that this would make state pensions unaffordable in the future. We talked through the
issues in detail and the fact that there are no easy answers here.

It is true that an ageing population is likely to put pressure on the current PAYG system, with
more money needed per member of the working population to fund those in retirement.
However, there is no simple alternative that provides a similar level of state pension without
putting a strain on our public finances.

In the meeting we did discuss the idea of moving from a PAYG system to a fully funded one.
This would mean that rather than the current workforce paying for the current retired
population, each generation would fund their own pensions. The key differences would be:

   A proportion of an individual’s tax would be saved and invested to provide their pension
    in retirement.
   The contributions required would be a function of the life expectancy, the retirement age
    and the expected investment returns between when contributions are made and pensions
    are paid.
   The level of the pension would likely still be fixed at point of retirement, therefore there
    would be a risk that if the assets were insufficient then the shortfall would need to be met
    by the state.

A funded scheme would stop the intergenerational cross subsidies inherent in the current
system and reduce the risk posed by the ageing population. However, a big challenge to
implementing this would be how we fund the current retired generation without the current
working population funding both the current retirees and their own pension (i.e. effectively
paying double).

The Minister of Finance has asked us to produce an article that the government can publish to
raise awareness of these issues with the general public in an unbiased way. There is concern
in the Treasury that the public will see newspaper articles such as the one in the Daily News
(the article I already shared with you) but will not understand the issue fully. In particular, the
public may not appreciate that the Treasury is taking these concerns seriously, or that the
Treasury is also being put in a difficult position. The article should include:

   background on the current system – the benefits available and how they are paid for.
   challenges of the current system, particularly in light of the expected ageing of the
    population.

Best wishes,
Roberta.


Attachment – Notes on pre-funding the state pension:

   A funded system benefits from the investment return over the period from the point at
    which the contributions are made to the point at which the pension is paid.
   Increases in life expectancy and the increases in the state pension to match inflation
    and/or average earnings, drive up the cost of the state pension.
   At present, the expected return on investments more than offsets the life expectancy and
    pension increases expected before retirement and therefore the theoretical cost of a
    funded state pension is lower than a PAYG arrangement.
   As life expectancy increases, the cost of a funded arrangement is expected to increase
    over time. However, the relative populations of working and retired people do not impact
    the cost and therefore the increases are not expected to be as extreme as under the PAYG
    system.
   It would take around 18% of the total tax income received by the government to fund
    their pensions for the future under a fully funded system.
   This would drop to 16% if the retirement age was increased to 70, or 12% if the
    retirement age was increased to 75.
