Details of your state pension
All citizens are eligible to receive a state pension from their 68th birthday. Details of the
benefit are given below:

   You must meet both of the following criteria to qualify for the state pension:
    o Be a citizen of Country X
    o Be a resident in Country X at the point you are claiming the pension. To be classed as
        a resident in Country X you must live in the country for at least 240 days of the year.
   The state pension is currently $132.85 per week.
   Pension payments are made weekly into a nominated bank account. Citizens must have a
    bank account to be able to claim the pension and no other arrangements will be
    considered.
   The level of the state pension is reviewed in September every year. The level of the
    increase is at the discretion of the government, but in the last 10 years it has increased at
    the higher of:
    o The National Inflation Index (NII), which tracks the change in average prices across
        the country
    o The National Wage Index (NWI), which tracks changes in average wages in the
        country.
    The increase is based on the change in the index over the preceding calendar year. For
    example, the increase in September 2024 would be based on the change in the index
    between 1 January 2023 and 31 December 2023.
   The state pension is the same for everyone above state pension age. It is not based on the
    amount of tax you have paid or reduced if you have other pension savings.

                     Country X Grapples with an Ageing Population:
                           Strain on Healthcare and Pensions

Country X is undergoing a profound transformation as its population continues to age at an
unprecedented rate, presenting both challenges and opportunities for its healthcare system
and pension programmes. The ‘silver tsunami’, or demographic ageing, is reshaping the
nation’s social and economic landscape, demanding innovative solutions and policy reforms
to ensure the well-being of its elderly citizens.

A demographic turning point

In recent decades, Country X has seen a remarkable increase in life expectancy and a decline
in birth rates, resulting in an ageing population. The proportion of citizens aged 68 and older
is now at an all-time high, and experts predict this trend will only intensify in the coming
years.

According to the latest census data, the elderly population (aged 68 and over) in Country X
accounts for over 20% of the total, a figure expected to rise significantly over the next
50 years. This shift has far-reaching implications for healthcare, pensions and the broader
economy.

Impact on healthcare

One of the most pressing challenges associated with an ageing population is the increased
demand for healthcare services. As people age, they tend to require more medical attention
and long-term care. The strain on Country X’s healthcare system is palpable, with hospitals
and clinics grappling with capacity issues and a shortage of healthcare professionals trained
to deal with geriatric patients.

Pensions under pressure

The surge in elderly citizens has also put a significant strain on the pension system. With
more retirees taking a pension from the state and a shrinking working-age population
contributing through their taxes, concerns about the sustainability of the system have grown.

State pension funding arrangements

Country X operates a Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) system to fund its state pension. Current
workers contribute to the system via their taxes, which are used to pay the retirement benefits
of current retirees. It operates on the principle that the current workforce financially supports
the retired population. When the current workers retire, the tax paid by the next generation of
workers will be used to pay their pensions.

PAYG systems lead to intergenerational transfers, with the current working generation
paying for the previous generation’s pension. Consequently, the current working generation
have the expectation that the generation after them will pay for their state pension when they
retire.

One of the key challenges with a PAYG system is demographic ageing. As populations age
and the proportion of retirees to workers increases (known as the dependency ratio), there can
be concerns about the sustainability of the system. Adequate funding must be available to
meet the pension obligations and adjustments may be needed to accommodate changing
demographics.

PAYG pension systems are just one of several approaches to providing retirement benefits.
Other systems, such as fully funded pension systems and hybrid systems that combine
elements of both PAYG and funded approaches, exist in different parts of the world. In a
fully funded system assets are accumulated in advance of retirement, with the aim that at the
point of retirement these are projected to be sufficient to pay for all future pension payments.
