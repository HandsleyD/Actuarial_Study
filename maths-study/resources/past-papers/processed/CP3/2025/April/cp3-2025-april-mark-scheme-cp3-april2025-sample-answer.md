# cp3-2025-april-mark-scheme-cp3-april2025-sample-answer

**23 April 2025**

**Overview of Country X state pensions, with current challenges**

This article has been produced by the Treasury to give the public an
introduction to the state pension and the challenges associated with
state pension funding.

**Introduction**

Country X provides a state pension to citizens over the age of 68. This
is paid for by the government from the taxes that it collects each year.
The proportion of the population over the age of 68 has been increasing
in recent years, and is expected to increase further in the future. This
means that the cost of providing the state pension is rising at a faster
rate than the tax that the government receives from working people. The
resulting increase in the proportion of tax being used to pay pensions
is a challenge for the government, with many other competing demands on
government spending. This article will cover:

- The basic features of the current state pension

- The challenges to the system coming from an ageing population

- An alternative way that the government could fund the state pension

- Other potential ways to mitigate the challenges the government is
  facing

**Basic features of the state pension**

All citizens resident in Country X over the age of 68 are eligible for a
state pension of \$132.85 per week. This amount increases each year at
the discretion of the government, but in recent years has always
increased at the higher of inflation and the change in average earnings.

The state pension is currently paid out of tax income, which means that
current taxpayers are funding the payments made to current pensioners.
Currently, the working age population is much larger than the retired
population, and the state pension is significantly lower than average
earnings. This makes paying for the pension from current taxation
feasible, and around 19% of tax received by the government is used to
pay the state pension.

**Challenges that come from an ageing population**

Country X is seeing significant increases in life expectancy, and birth
rates have been falling in recent years. This means that the balance
between the working population and the retired population is changing
over time, with more weighting towards the retired population. The
government currently expects this trend to continue over the next fifty
years. The following chart shows a projection of the proportions up
until 2080.

<img src="media/image1.png" style="width:5.775in;height:3.39167in" />

In 2020 there are around 3 working age people for every one retired
person. By 2080 this is expected to halve, with only 1.5 working age
people per retired person.

To ensure that state pensions can still be paid, the proportion of tax
revenue used to pay for state pensions is projected to rise from 19% to
just over 40% by 2080. This is a significant increase and means that the
government will not have as much to spend on other services in Country
X.

**An alternative funding system**

One alternative to funding the pensions from the tax paid by current
workers would be for each generation to fund their own state pension.
The government could invest a proportion of the tax raised from working
people pay and save it to pay for their pensions in the future. This has
two benefits over the current system:

1.  The invested money would grow with investment returns. If this
    growth exceeds the increases to the state pension over the period
    from when the contribution is made to when the pension is paid this
    will reduce the cost of providing state pensions.

2.  Each generation would pay for their own pension, and therefore there
    would be no cross subsidy between generations. The relative sizes of
    the working and retired populations would therefore not have such an
    impact.

If the current working population were to fund their own state pension
it is projected to cost around 18% of the tax they pay. This compares to
19% of tax used to fund pensions in the current system.

However, a significant challenge arises from changing to this system.
The government has not set aside any funds to pay pensions for the
current retired population. If taxation from the current workforce is
set aside to pay for their future pensions, rather than pay pensions for
current retired population, then the government must find alternative
funding for the current pensioners. This change would effectively double
the cost of providing state pensions in the short term.

**Other ways to mitigate the challenges**

There are ways to address the challenges posed by the aging population
in Country X within the current pension arrangements. If we can increase
the proportion of the working age population relative to the retired
population then we stabilise the proportion of tax needed to pay
pensions. One way to achieve this is to increase the retirement age.
This increases the period that citizens are in work, and therefore
increases the contributions they make. It also reduces the number of
years that a pension will be paid, reducing the cost. Increasing the
contributions and reducing the cost of the pension will bring down the
proportion of tax needed to pay for the state pension.

Another approach would be to increase the working population through
other means. For example, if Country X could increase immigration at
working ages, it could increase the tax paid and help pay the state
pension bill. This would only work, however, if the increase in
immigration was matched by an increase in employment.

Summary

There are challenges to funding the state pension system in Country X
over the coming decades. Increasing life expectancy and falling
birth-rates mean that the proportion of tax needed to pay for the state
pension will increase significantly.

Moving to a funded system, where each generation pays for their own
pension, and the money is invested between being collected as tax and
paid as pension could lead to lower costs in the long term. However,
pensions for the current retired population would still need to be
funded somehow. Alternatively, increasing the retirement age, or
increasing the working population through higher immigration could also
help to alleviate the challenges of an aging population.

There are no easy solutions to this issue. The government is taking
these concerns seriously and government departments are considering
actions that can be taken to mitigate the challenges.

Question 2

i\)

This article is aimed at the general public. We should aim our writing
at the least sophisticated reader, to ensure that everyone understands
the key points that we are making. As such our audience has the
following characteristics:

- No knowledge of the current state pension, including the retirement
  age, the amount of the state pension, and who is eligible to receive
  it.

- No prior understanding of how the state pension is funded.

- Not necessarily an understanding of the demographic change that is
  expected in Country X, or the implications this might have on funding
  pensions. The email request mentions the Daily News article, and so
  the article drafted here needs to be accessible to people who have
  read that (or similar) articles and so have some idea of the issues of
  an ageing population, but it should also be accessible to those for
  whom this is the first they have heard of the issues raised.

- An interest / concern in the government finances and the
  sustainability of the economic reality of Country X

ii\) I took the following steps to ensure that my answer was aimed at
the audience:

- Explained the basics of the state pension to ensure everyone
  understood what state benefits were provided and at what age.

- Explained from first principles the funding of the state pension. I
  explained this in a way that meant that there was no need for any
  prior understanding of pension funding of government finances.

- Put the state pensions in context with the rest of the benefits and
  services that the state provides – I made it clear that currently
  pensions account for 19% of the tax revenue that the state receives.
  This helps to put the issue in context – this is a significant
  proportion of government expenditure and if it doubles that is likely
  to be a large issue.

- Gave an introduction to the demographic dynamics for Country X –
  covering the ageing population; specifically the change in proportions
  between the working and retired populations. For anyone not aware of
  these issues this gives the background to why there is an issue.

- Illustrated the proportions within the working and retired populations
  in a chart to give a visualisation of the scale of the issue. For
  anyone new to the topic this helps to show that there is a material
  change in the relative populations without overstating it and making
  the issue seem unsolvable.

- Explained the concept of a funded pension from first principles, not
  assuming any prior knowledge.

- Explained why changing the retirement age and increasing the
  working-age population through immigration could help to solve the
  issue in a way that required no prior knowledge but focussed only on
  the issue here and did not bring in wider considerations. This article
  is narrow in its scope and it is important to only focus on these
  issues in relation to the demographic challenges and their effect on
  the pension system.

iii\)

We have been asked to draft an unbiased article. For this purpose this
means

- Where possible we should present facts, and not commentary about these
  facts

- Potential solutions should be presented with their key merits and
  disadvantages outlined

- No one solution should be favoured in the article, because no
  strategic decisions have yet been taken.

- The article should represent the current thinking of the treasury, and
  while it should make clear that the treasury is concerned with these
  issues, it should acknowledge the issue and recognise its
  significance.

- The article should give a balanced account of the challenges – it
  should not overstate them and encourage panic or give an unnecessarily
  bleak view of the future. However, it should not understate their
  relevance to society, or the difficult choices that the country faces
  in funding future state pensions.

iv\)

I have taken the following steps to ensure that the article is unbiased:

- The introduction is entirely factual – I set out basic facts about the
  pension and the demographic makeup of the country. This helps to set
  the tone that this is not an opinion article but one giving unbiased
  facts.

- The section outlining the features of the state pension and how it is
  funded is again only presenting facts, underlining that this is a
  paper outlining factual information.

- I make it clear that the future projections are the government’s
  projections. This makes it clear that there is uncertainty (e.g.
  talking about what the government ‘expects this trend’ to do) but it
  is transparent that there is uncertainty and that this is the
  government’s current expectation.

- When presenting the funded option, I made it clear that this was only
  one possible option and there were others. I also talked about both
  the benefits and the challenges with this approach.

- The other alternatives are presented only in relation to their impact
  on the state pension affordability issue. Increasing the state pension
  age or increasing immigration might both be controversial, but to
  avoid getting into all of the issues I only presented the impact that
  they would have on state pension funding.

- I summarised by making it clear that the government was taking the
  concerns seriously but that there were no easy options and it was
  considering all of these options.

v\)

- This article is aimed at the general public, therefore any visual aids
  need to be very intuitive and simple. I presented the progression of
  the proportion of retired to working population over the time as this
  is a very simple concept.

- The chart shows a very clear trend, which can be grasped at a glance.
  This helps support my message that there is a change that will
  materialise over the coming years.

- I chose to show the percentage rather than the actual population –
  showing the actual population could have given the same message, but
  the general total population trend would have been visible (i.e. the
  bars would have been increasing over time as well as the proportions
  changing). This would have detracted from the key message, which is
  that the changing proportions are causing the issue.

- I restricted the data to show the position now and into the future.
  The past is not too relevant for this topic, and so I wanted to show a
  clear trend from the current decade.

- I kept the titles and legend very simple and clear, and ensured that
  the chart had easy-to-read and simple labels on axes, to make
  interpreting it easier for those not used to interpreting charts.
