1    Company A is a small health and care insurer specialising in accelerated and
     standalone Critical Illness (CI) insurance products. The Marketing Director of
     Company A has proposed to incorporate tiered benefits into these products.

     (i)      Define tiered benefits in the context of CI insurance products.           [3]

     (ii)     Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the Marketing Director’s
              proposal from the perspective of Company A.                               [5]

     (iii)    Describe the factors that Company A should consider before incorporating
              tiered benefits into its CI product features.                            [6]

     The Marketing Director has further proposed to add a benefit increase option to
     Company A’s CI product features. This option is only available at the policy inception
     date where the sum assured, and the premiums, will automatically increase at a fixed
     rate of 5% p.a. at each subsequent policy anniversary.

     (iv)     Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the Marketing Director’s further
              proposal from the perspective of Company A.                               [4]

     Company A’s management is concerned that these additional product features
     introduce further operational complexities. They have therefore suggested that the
     existing information technology systems and models should be replaced by improved
     systems.

     (v)      Describe the investigations and criteria that Company A should consider
              before deciding to replace the following systems:

              •   pricing system and model
              •   policy administration system
              •   valuation system and model.

2    Country X is a developed country with an established health and care insurance
     market. Country X’s current solvency capital requirements framework has the
     following key features:

     •      The minimum capital requirement applicable to all insurers is a fixed monetary
            amount.
     •      For capital purposes, prudent reductions are applied to value of assets other than
            cash and cash equivalents.
     •      Insurance companies are required to perform a formal solvency assessment
            annually. Failure to meet the required minimum level of solvency will result in
            regulatory actions such as more frequent solvency reporting, requirement for the
            insurance companies to put in place a plan to improve its solvency, or withdrawal
            of authorisation in the most serious cases.

     (i)       Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the current solvency capital
               requirements framework from the perspective of:

               (a)     health and care insurers in Country X.

               (b)     Country X’s regulatory authority.
                                                                                            [8]

     Country X’s regulatory authority has proposed the following changes to the solvency
     capital requirements framework, to be implemented in 3 years’ time:

     •      In addition to the minimum capital requirement, a solvency capital requirement
            based on the company’s own risk profile will apply.
     •      Reductions are no longer applied to value of assets. Asset risks will be modelled
            and quantified explicitly through the market risk capital component of the
            solvency capital requirements.
     •      Solvency assessments are to be performed quarterly.
     •      Reporting of reserves and capital requirements will be required at a more granular
            level split by main product types.

     Company B is a Private Medical Insurance (PMI) provider in Country X. Company
     B’s product range covers individual PMI and group PMI products. The individual
     PMIs are 1-year renewable, while the group PMIs are 3-year renewable products.

     Company B’s management has expressed concerns about the financial and practical
     impact of the new framework. They wish to investigate the impact of the changes on
     the financial results and the actions required to ensure compliance with the new
     framework.

     (ii)      Discuss the key factors that should be considered in respect of:

               (a)     assessing the impact on Company B’s financial results.

               (b)     a project plan to implement the required actions.

3    Company C is a large health and care insurance company in Country Y. It specialises
     in Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI) products and is a market leader in Country Y.
     Its LTCI products include both pre-funded and immediate needs plans. Neither plans
     currently offer a surrender value.

     The Marketing Director of Company C has proposed to introduce an option for future
     LTCI policyholders to terminate their LTCI contracts in return of a surrender value.

     (i)       Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the Marketing Director’s
               proposal from the perspective of Company C.                                [4]

     (ii)      Set out the approach, including methodology and assumptions, which
               Company C could take to evaluate the surrender values for both pre-funded
               and immediate needs LTCI plans.                                           [8]

     The Marketing Director has also proposed to offer the surrender value option to
     existing LTCI policyholders.

     (iii)     Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this additional proposal from the
               perspective of Company C.                                                  [5]

     The Marketing Director further proposed that the following options could be offered
     to the policyholders instead of terminating their LTCI contracts.

     For pre-funded LTCI:

     •      paid-up premium pre-claims
     •      partial surrender pre-claims
     •      on making a claim, take a proportion of the benefit as a cash lump sum
     •      partial surrender while the claim is in payment.

     For immediate needs LTCI:

     •      partial surrender.

     (iv)      Discuss the factors that Company C would need to consider before offering
               these additional options.                                                 [6]

4    Company D is a large health and care insurance company in Country Z specialising in
     Private Medical Insurance (PMI) products. The company’s market share has reached
     over 70% of Country Z’s PMI market through competitive pricing and acquisitions of
     other PMI insurers.

     The regulatory authority of Country Z is growing increasingly concerned over
     Company D’s dominance in the market. It has recently proposed to introduce a
     framework allowing hospitals and health and care providers to offer ‘access package’
     plans directly to customers. Under these plans, customers will pay a fixed monthly
     contribution to gain access to a package of health and care services from their chosen
     hospitals or health and care providers. These plans will not be treated as insurance
     products and are therefore not subject to Country Z’s insurance regulatory
     requirements.

     (i)      Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this proposal from the
              perspective of Country Z’s regulatory authority.                            [4]

     (ii)     Suggest other actions that Country Z’s regulatory authority could take to
              address its concerns over Company D’s dominance in the local market. [10]
