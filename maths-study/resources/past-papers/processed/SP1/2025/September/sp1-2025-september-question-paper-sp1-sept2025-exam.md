1     Company A is a health and care insurance company operating in Country X. Country
      Y is a neighbouring country to Country X with very similar but not identical language
      and culture as Country X. Company A’s management is proposing to enter Country
      Y’s insurance market by setting up an insurance company in Country Y. This
      insurance company will specialise in selling Critical Illness (CI) products to group
      schemes sponsored by Country Y’s employers. Company A is carrying out
      preliminary investigations into whether Country Y will be a suitable market to enter.

      (i)       Describe the main features of a group CI product.                              [3]

      (ii)      Suggest possible reasons why Company A would want to set up an insurance
                company in Country Y and specialise in selling group CI products.      [5]

      (iii)     Discuss the factors that Company A should consider before deciding whether
                to proceed with the proposal to set up an insurance company in Country Y.
                                                                                         [10]

      Company A’s preliminary investigations identified the following facts:

            The overall (i.e. all critical illnesses combined) morbidity experience over the last
             20 years had been lower in Country Y than Country X.
            Historical inflation rates had been much lower in Country Y than Country X in the
             last 5 years.
            The average salary in Country Y is marginally higher than Country X.

      (iv)      Discuss how Company A may make use of these facts in the following
                processes for the group CI products to be sold in Country Y:

                (a)     product design

                (b)     underwriting

                (c)     pricing.

3     Company C is a large health and care insurance company that sells Long Term Care
      Insurance (LTCI) in Country X. The company sells both pre-funded and immediate
      needs versions of LTCI.

      (i)       Describe briefly the differences between the main product features of
                pre-funded LTCI and immediate needs LTCI.                                   [3]

      The results of Company C’s recent experience investigation show that over the last
      5 years the pre-funded LTCI product sold lower levels of new business than the
      immediate needs LTCI product.

      (ii)      Suggest possible reasons for this experience.                               [5]

      In a recent budget statement, the Minister of Finance of Country X highlighted the
      following expectations for the government’s 5-year outlook for Country X:

            Annual gross domestic product growth is expected to be significantly lower over
             the next 5 years compared to the last 5 years.
            Annual inflation is expected to be significantly higher over the next 5 years
             compared to the last 5 years.
            The government expects to reduce direct spending on social services for the
             elderly and instead, promote private financing of health and care insurance
             for the elderly.
            The average life expectancy is expected to increase over the next 5 years from
             75 years to at least 80 years.
            The government will investigate increasing the state pension age for citizens of
             Country X.

      (iii)     Discuss the opportunities and risks to Company C of each of the above
                expectations.                                                             [12]

      (iv)      Suggest possible actions that Company C could take to mitigate the risks
                identified in part (iii).                                                  [8]

4     Company D is a large health and care insurance company in Country Y, specialising
      in Private Medical Insurance (PMI) products. Company D’s marketing director has
      proposed that a new PMI product is designed specifically to meet the needs of
      Country Y’s athletes across field, track and long-distance disciplines. The marketing
      director’s proposal is based on the following observations:

         Country Y’s recent success in winning gold medals by local athletes in
          international events including the Olympics and the World Championships has
          increased the number of citizens aspiring to pursue a career in athletics.
         Country Y’s existing PMI products are not tailored for the specific needs of
          athletes because of the higher level of injuries requiring specialist treatment and
          the need for medical cover at overseas events where products have territorial
          limits.
         Athletes are role models in society and can be used by Company D for marketing
          purposes to enhance its public image.

      Discuss the factors that Company D should consider before agreeing to the marketing
      director’s proposal.                                                            [12]

5     Company E is a health and care insurance company that sells a full range of health
      and care products in Country Z.

      Country Z’s current regulatory regime requires health and care insurance companies
      to calculate their statutory reserves using prudent assumptions (best estimate
      assumptions plus a margin for prudence), plus additional capital requirements using
      simple formula based on written premiums.

      Country Z’s regulatory regime is currently being changed. The proposed changes
      include:

         The reserves will be calculated using best estimate assumptions.
         A separate risk margin will be calculated instead of a margin for prudence.
          o The risk margin will be calculated using the ‘cost of capital’ approach on top
              of the best estimate reserves.
          o This involves first projecting forward the future capital that the company is
              required to hold in respect of these risks, where this is determined at the end of
              each projection period (e.g. year) during the run-off of the business.
          o The projected capital is determined according to the relevant regulatory basis
              and is the amount required to be held in excess of the projected liabilities at
              each period.
          o These projected capital amounts are then multiplied by a cost of capital rate.
         In addition to the best estimate reserves and risk margin, additional solvency
          capital requirements will be required.
          o The level of solvency capital requirements will be based on either a risk
              measure approach such as ‘value at risk’ where the insurers will apply their
              own judgement when setting the parameters, or a standard formula approach
              where Country Z’s regulator will prescribe the methodology and parameters to
              be used by the insurers.

      Discuss the factors Company E should take into consideration in assessing the
      potential implications of Country Z’s regulatory changes.                            [10]
