1     Company A is a health and care insurance company that is developing a new
      individual Critical Illness (CI) product with guaranteed premiums throughout the
      policy term. Company A will carry out full medical underwriting for the new CI
      product.

      (i)     Describe the full medical underwriting process that Company A will carry out
              for its new CI product.                                                  [10]

      Company A is considering adding Guaranteed Insurability Options (GIOs) to its new
      CI product. One GIO under consideration is an option that allows the policyholder to
      extend the policy term on the original expiry date at Company A’s standard premium
      rates without supplying further medical evidence.

      (ii)    Set out the approach that Company A could take to price the GIO to extend
              the policy term.                                                          [7]

      A second GIO under consideration is an option that allows the policyholder to
      increase the sum assured when a particular life event (e.g. on marriage or buying a
      new house) occurs, which is priced using Company A’s standard premium rates
      without supplying further medical evidence.

      (iii)   Explain how the pricing considerations for the option to increase the sum
              assured may differ from the option to extend the policy term.                [3]

4     Company A is a medium-sized health and care insurance company that was launched
      5 years ago, specialising in individual and group Income Protection (IP) products.

      The current reinsurance arrangements for Company A are as follows:

            Individual IP:
             o Quota share treaty arrangement on a risk premium basis, with a retention level
                 of 0% (i.e. 100% of the morbidity risk is reinsured).
             o Facultative reinsurance arrangements that are individually negotiable on a
                 case-by-case basis.

            Group IP:
             o Excess of loss treaty with an excess point of £100,000 per life and £1 million
                per scheme. There is an upper limit of £5 million per scheme.
             o Aggregate excess of loss with an excess point of £20 million.
             o Catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance.

      (i)       Set out the rationale for the specific reinsurance arrangements mentioned
                above, from the perspective of Company A.                                 [10]

      Company A has experienced significant business growth over the last 5 years. The
      Finance Director has proposed the following changes to Company A’s reinsurance
      strategy for all future business written under its individual IP portfolio:

            Increase the quota share retention level from 0% to 50% (i.e. 50% of morbidity
             risk will be reinsured).
            ‘Nil reinsurance premium period’ where for each new policy reinsured, free
             reinsurance cover is to be provided in the first 2 years, followed by level
             reinsurance premiums thereafter.

      (ii)      Discuss the possible rationale behind the Finance Director’s proposals.       [8]

      (iii)     Describe the investigations that Company A should take to assess the
                appropriateness of increasing the retention level from 0% to 50%.          [6]

5     Company B is a large bancassurer that sells a range of health and care products
      including conventional (non-linked) and unit-linked Critical Illness (CI) insurance
      products.

      For the conventional CI product, premiums are guaranteed throughout the policy
      term. CI benefit for each individual policyholder is the amount of sum assured
      selected at the start of the policy term. This product does not offer a surrender benefit
      if the policyholders terminate their policies before the end of the policy term.

      For the unit-linked CI product, policyholders can choose from a range of Company
      B’s unit-linked funds (e.g. equity, property or fixed interest security funds) to invest
      their premiums to build up their individual unit funds. Investment returns on
      policyholders’ unit funds are directly linked to the investment performance of
      Company B’s unit-linked funds. The CI benefit for each individual policyholder is
      either the amount of sum assured selected by the policyholders at the start of the
      policy term or the value of the unit fund at the claim inception date, whichever is
      higher. This product also offers a surrender benefit equivalent to the value of the unit
      fund at the termination date without a surrender penalty. Morbidity charges are
      deducted from policyholders’ unit funds to cover the cost of the CI benefits.
      Additional expense charges are deducted to cover the cost of administration. Both
      morbidity and expense charges are reviewable, with the first review on each policy’s
      fifth anniversary, which are followed by annual reviews thereafter.

      (i)     Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each type of CI product from the
              perspective of Company B.                                                 [8]

      (ii)    Set out the factors that Company B should consider when reviewing the
              morbidity charges for its unit-linked CI products.                      [10]
