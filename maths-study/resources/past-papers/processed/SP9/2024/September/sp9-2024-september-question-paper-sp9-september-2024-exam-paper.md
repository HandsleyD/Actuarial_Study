      As part of enhancing the risk register, ABC is considering adding additional fields to

      (iv)    List six additional risk register fields that could be included in the risk register.

      It has been discovered that four fraudulent invoices were submitted to the Finance
      Manager over the past 3 months. The total value of the fraudulent invoices was
      $12,000. All four invoices were paid by the Finance Manager, who followed all
      processes and checks before making payment. The Finance Manager first noticed the
      transactions were unusual when producing the annual accounts. The Finance Manager
      immediately raised their concerns directly with the CEO.

      (vi)    Explain, with reference to risk management best practices, how the CEO
              should respond to the concerns raised by the Finance Manager.                    [5]

      The CEO commissioned an investigation into the fraudulent invoices. The
      investigation highlighted that the Finance Manager had followed all the relevant
      processes and checks for payment of the invoices. Email approvals for these invoices
      had been received from the CEO and the CFO. However, both the CEO and the
      CFO’s email addresses had been compromised, so the required approvals had been
      sent fraudulently. Fraudulent access to email accounts is a separate risk event in the
      risk register and both the CEO and CFO had followed the controls as set out in the
      risk register for this risk event.

      One of your fellow trustees argues that there is no need to take any further action as a
      result of this incident, on the basis that all relevant risks were included in the risk
      register, and all controls set out in the risk register were followed at all times.

      (vii)   Discuss the trustee’s argument set out above.                                    [4]

      The trustees of ABC have been discussing what an appropriate level of cash reserves
      for ABC may be.

      (viii) Suggest four distinct reasons why a charity should hold reserves.                 [2]

      Some of ABC’s trustees are concerned that the current level of cash reserves available
      to ABC is too low. The trustees have asked the CFO to draft a policy to define an
      acceptable level of cash reserves for ABC to hold.

      The CFO’s first draft of the policy is the following statement:

      ‘ABC should have at least $20k in cash at all times’.

2     Country Y has experienced a long period of low investment in its infrastructure. In
      particular, railways and housing stock in Country Y are in need of repair and
      expansion. Country Y’s government is considering what actions it should take.

      (i)     Set out how the government could apply enterprise risk management
              principles to identify and manage risks associated with Country Y’s
              infrastructure.                                                               [8]

      The government is aware that a significant proportion of the assets issued in Country
      Y are held by insurance companies based in Country Y, in respect of investments and
      pension savings products. Insurance companies in Country Y have, for several years,
      adopted an investment policy of holding their assets in equities and corporate bonds
      issued in Country Y.

      (ii)    Identify the key risks to the insurance companies arising from their
              investments in equities and corporate bonds in Country Y.                     [4]

      The government decides to carry out a consultation. The consultation proposes to
      incentivise insurance companies to invest in Country Y’s infrastructure, rather than
      equities and corporate bonds. The incentives will be applied via changes to the
      regulatory risk capital regime. As part of its consultation, the government seeks input
      from a panel of board members of Country Y’s largest insurance companies,
      including their CEOs and Chief Risk Officers (CROs).

      (iii)   Discuss the different perspectives of the insurance companies’ CEOs and
              CROs in relation to the proposal.                                             [8]

      Insurance companies in Country Y are subject to regulatory oversight by the
      regulator. The regulator requires insurance companies to hold regulatory risk capital
      according to regulations that are similar to the Solvency II framework in the European
      Union.

      The regulator in Country Y is also participating in the government’s consultation. The
      regulator is concerned that the government’s proposals will increase the risks that the
      insurance companies are exposed to.

      (iv)    Discuss the key concerns that the regulator may have.                       [10]

      Some actuaries from insurance companies in Country Y have formed a working group
      on behalf of the Actuarial Association in Country Y. The purpose of the working
      group is to consider the implications of the consultation on regulatory capital
      modelling and risk aggregation, and to produce a report.

      (v)     Discuss the key points that the actuaries should include in their report.    [10]
