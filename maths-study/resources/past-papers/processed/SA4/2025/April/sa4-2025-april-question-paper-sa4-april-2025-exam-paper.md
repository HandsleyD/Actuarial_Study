1    A defined benefit pension scheme has just completed a triennial valuation. The
     balance sheet on the funding and solvency bases is shown below:

                                             Funding          Solvency
                                              (£m)              (£m)
                   Assets                     1,350            1,350

                   Liabilities
                   Actives                     50                55
                   Deferreds                  500               700
                   Pensioners                 800               850
                   Expenses                                      15
                   Total                      1,350            1,620
                   Funding level              100%             83%

     The scheme’s trustees are pleased that the scheme has finally achieved a funding level
     of 100% on the funding basis after years of being in deficit. They now want to know
     more about the process of securing the scheme’s benefits with an insurer. As part of
     this they would like to understand the difference between the funding and solvency
     bases.

     (i)      Discuss why the liabilities determined on a solvency basis are generally
              greater than the liabilities determined on a funding basis.                     [8]

     The trustees do not understand why the ratio of the solvency to funding liabilities is
     highest for the deferreds and lowest for the pensioners.

     (ii)     Explain the relative differences in solvency and funding liabilities for the three
              different members’ classes.                                                    [6]

     The trustees would like to move towards being fully funded on a solvency basis, with
     the aim of insuring the scheme’s liabilities within the next 10 years.

     (iii)    Set out the strategies and actions that the trustees could take over the next
              10 years to help the scheme achieve this target.                              [12]

     A year later the trustees have asked for an updated solvency position and this is
     shown in the table below:

                                               Solvency (£m)
                               Assets              1,470

                               Liabilities
                               Actives                  45
                               Deferreds               560
                               Pensioners              850
                               Expenses                 15
                               Total                  1,470

     (iv)     Suggest reasons for the improvement in the solvency position.                   [8]

     Having seen the updated solvency position, the scheme’s sponsor has proposed that
     all benefits are secured with an insurer as soon as possible. The sponsor has asked the
     trustees to ensure that the scheme is in a good position to approach an insurer.

     (v)      Set out the main preparatory steps the trustees will need to take in advance of
              approaching insurers. Your answer should include consideration of:

              (a)    data and benefits.

              (b)    other issues – including legal, planning and practical issues.
                                                                                          [10]

     The trustees have secured a buy-in with an insurer through the purchase of annuity
     contracts for all of the scheme benefits. The buy-in premium was £80 million less
     than the assets in the scheme. The sponsor currently pays all scheme expenses and has
     asked the trustees if expenses could, from now on, be paid directly from the scheme,
     such that there is no financial obligation from the sponsor to meet the cost of the
     scheme’s future expenses.

     (vi)     Outline the points you would consider when preparing your advice to the
              trustees regarding this request.                                             [4]

     At a meeting with the trustees, the sponsor has proposed that on wind-up of the
     scheme, any remaining surplus should be returned to the sponsor. The trustees
     respond by proposing that the surplus should be split equally, with 50% paid to the
     employer and 50% being used to enhance members’ benefits.

     (vii)    Discuss the factors to be considered in determining how the scheme surplus
              should be split between the employer and the members.                      [6]

2    An occupational pension scheme in a developed country has recently experienced a
     significant increase in member requests for transfer values.

     (i)      List reasons why pension scheme members may request a transfer value.           [2]

     (ii)     Suggest possible reasons why this scheme may be experiencing an increase in
              transfer value requests.                                                 [4]

     The scheme has recently closed to future accrual and all active members were
     provided with illustrative transfer values. At the last funding valuation, the
     contribution rate for active members was set at 25% in the schedule of contributions.

     A member who had 5 years’ service in the scheme has been provided with a transfer
     value of £20,000. The member has complained that the transfer value is significantly
     less than the amount they had calculated, based on their current earnings of £40,000,
     length of service and the 25% contribution rate. They have written to the trustees
     asking for an explanation.

     (iii)    Discuss the possible reasons for the difference between the transfer value and
              the member’s calculation.                                                    [8]

     The scheme’s trustees are considering insuring the scheme’s benefits through a buy-in
     with an insurer. They have asked their actuary to present a report outlining the
     advantages and disadvantages of a buy-in for the main stakeholders.

     (iv)     Outline the main points that the actuary would include in their report, from the
              point of view of:

              (a)    the trustees.

              (b)    the members.

              (c)    the sponsoring employer.
                                                                                              [8]

     The trustees have decided to proceed with the buy-in and have received premium
     quotations from several insurers. The quotation amounts are all very similar. The
     trustees have asked their actuary to recommend additional criteria, other than the
     premium, to be considered in the selection process.

     (v)      Outline the points that the actuary would include in their reply to the trustees.
                                                                                             [5]

     The trustees have now selected an insurer and the buy-in is in progress. Once the
     buy-in is completed the insurer will use its own factors for member options. The
     insurer will provide a document to the trustees with illustrative factors that will be
     used post buy-in.

     (vi)     (a)    List the factors you would expect to be included.                        [2]

              (b)    Discuss how the factors in part (vi)(a) may differ from those currently
                     used by the scheme.                                                   [7]
