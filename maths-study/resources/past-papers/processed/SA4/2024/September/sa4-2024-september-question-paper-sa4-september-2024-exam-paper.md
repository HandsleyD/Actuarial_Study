1    A pension scheme established under trust has two sections as follows:

           DB section, providing a range of Defined Benefit (DB) pensions and lump sums.
           DC section, providing Defined Contribution (DC) benefits.

     One of the scheme’s trustees has recently become concerned that the benefits
     provided are not equal across genders, which may place the scheme at risk of
     discrimination claims.

     (i)       State eight distinct reasons why one scheme member’s benefits may differ
               from another’s, stating, for each reason, whether it is relevant to the DB
               section, DC section or both.                                               [8]

     The trustees have asked an actuary to determine if there are gender differences in the
     benefits provided by the DB and DC sections.

     (ii)      Describe the investigations that the actuary should undertake, together with a
               summary of the data or information needed to carry out the exercise.        [14]

     (iii)     Outline, with reference to your answer in part (i), actions that could be taken
               to remove or mitigate the impact of gender imbalance in the scheme benefits,
               by:

                  the trustees, in relation to the operation of the pension scheme.
                  the sponsoring employer, in relation to its employment policy.

2    A defined benefit pension scheme uses fixed, rather than market-related, commutation
     factors for converting a portion of members’ pensions into a lump sum at retirement.
     These factors are reviewed at each funding valuation and remain fixed between
     valuations.

     Following the latest valuation, the trustees are updating the scheme’s commutation
     factors. However, due to a recent period of significant volatility in market yields, they
     have expressed concern about the risks of using fixed factors.

     (i)       Outline the advantages and disadvantages of using commutation factors that
               are:

               (a)     fixed for a period.

               (b)     based on market yields at or near retirement.
                                                                                             [8]

     The trustees have decided to undertake a full review of the scheme’s factors and to
     use market-related commutation factors in the future. They have asked their actuary to
     provide advice in relation to the new factors.

     (ii)      Set out the points the actuary should include in their advice regarding:

               (a)     setting the key assumptions underlying the commutation factors.      [11]

               (b)     the options for calculating a factor that allows for market conditions
                       and a proposed choice for this scheme with the rationale for that
                       choice.                                                                [3]

     The trustees have asked their actuary to produce a report to discuss the practicalities
     of implementing the new commutation factors and if any existing administrative
     procedures need to be revised, in particular:

           the timing of benefit information packs automatically issued to members as they
            approach retirement.
           the guarantee period for benefit quotations provided.
           equity between members whose information packs were issued before, versus
            after, the implementation.
           communications to members now, explaining the change.

     (iii)     Set out, with reasons, the points the actuary should include in their report. [12]

3    A developed country has an established occupational pensions market including
     Defined Benefit (DB) and Defined Contribution (DC) schemes. The government
     wishes to stimulate economic growth by increasing investment in the following
     assets:

           Private equity companies funding local infrastructure projects
           The equity shares of local companies.

     To achieve this, the government is proposing to require pension schemes to invest a
     minimum proportion of funds held into these assets. The proposal is currently under
     consultation and is as follows:

           DB proposal: Invest a minimum proportion of all assets.
           For DC schemes: EITHER Proposal 1 – invest a minimum proportion of all
            available member funds; OR Proposal 2 – invest a minimum proportion of the
            default fund used when members have not made a fund choice.

     (i)       Describe the advantages and disadvantages of the government’s proposal from
               the perspectives of:

               (a)     the government.

               (b)     trustees and/or managers of pension schemes.

               (c)     scheme sponsors.
                                                                                            [8]

     Scheme A is a mature DB occupational pension scheme that is closed to future
     accrual and has no benefits linked to salary increases. Scheme A’s trustees have been
     following a journey towards securing all benefits with an insurance company with a
     target end date in 5 years’ time, at which point Scheme A will be fully funded on a
     buy-out basis. This date was chosen to ensure the required contributions from the
     sponsor, which has a strong covenant but has cashflow constraints, are kept at a
     modest level.

     Scheme A’s trustees have asked their actuary to produce a report on:

           the impact that the government proposals might have on Scheme A’s journey
            towards buyout.
           what actions the trustees might take to mitigate this impact.

     (ii)      Set out the points that Scheme A’s actuary might include in their report.   [15]
