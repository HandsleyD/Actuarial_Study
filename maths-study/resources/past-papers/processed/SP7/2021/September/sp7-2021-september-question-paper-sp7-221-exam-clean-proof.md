2     Company J was set up a few years ago as an online-only insurer, selling property and
      liability insurance to Small-to-Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in the country it operates
      in.

      Internet penetration in the country is low, so Company J is considering using two
      large brokers to sell its insurance policies to better reach its target market. It plans to
      start offering around 40% of its current business through these two brokers.

      The Chief Distribution Officer has mentioned that the premium and claims
      information will be received through quarterly bordereaux.

      (i)     Describe two possible impacts of the change in distribution channel on the
              incurred claims information received by Company J’s claims department. [2]

      (ii)    Discuss the impact of this change in distribution on Company J’s IBNR
              estimation.                                                               [5]

                                                                                         [2]

      (ii)    Outline the advantages of estimating IBNER at an individual claim level.          [2]

      Company T is a large travel insurer with an average annual premium income of
      $2 billion.

      A Reserving Actuary has recently joined the team from a smaller insurance company
      with an average annual premium of $300 million, writing Medical Malpractice
      insurance for hospitals.

      In their previous organisation, the Reserving Actuary was estimating the IBNER
      reserves at an individual claim level by analysing patterns in the settlement delay of
      historical claims of a similar nature, and has proposed that the same approach be used
      for Company T.

      The Chief Actuary at Company T disagrees with the idea, saying it is ‘an impractical
      approach’.

      (iii)   Discuss the arguments for and against the Chief Actuary’s statement.       [8]

      (iii)   Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of holding claims reserves in the

8     Company A is a specialised reinsurance company selling per risk Excess of Loss (XoL)
      reinsurance to insurers requiring reinsurance coverage for their Commercial Property,
      Personal Accident, and General Liability Lines of Business (LoB) under a single

      It offers these covers on both a facultative and treaty basis and the policy also
      provides coverage for clash events.

      The cover must be purchased as a single policy, and a breakdown of premium by LoB
