Introduction

The Examiners’ Report is written by the Chief Examiner with the aim of helping candidates,
both those who are sitting the examination for the first time and using past papers as a
revision aid and also those who have previously failed the subject.

The Examiners are charged by Council with examining the published syllabus. The
Examiners have access to the Core Reading, which is designed to interpret the syllabus, and
will generally base questions around it but are not required to examine the content of Core
Reading specifically or exclusively.

For numerical questions the Examiners’ preferred approach to the solution is reproduced in
this report; other valid approaches are given appropriate credit. For essay-style questions,
particularly the open-ended questions in the later subjects, the report may contain more points
than the Examiners will expect from a solution that scores full marks.

For some candidates, this may be their first attempt at answering an examination using open
books and online. The Examiners expect all candidates to have a good level of knowledge
and understanding of the topics and therefore candidates should not be overly dependent on
open book materials. In our experience, candidates that spend too long researching answers
in their materials will not be successful either because of time management issues or because
they do not properly answer the questions.

Many candidates rely on past exam papers and examiner reports. Great caution must be
exercised in doing so because each exam question is unique. As with all professional
examinations, it is insufficient to repeat points of principle, formula or other textbook works.
The examinations are designed to test “higher order” thinking including candidates’ ability to
apply their knowledge to the facts presented in detail, synthesise and analyse their findings,
and present conclusions or advice. Successful candidates concentrate on answering the
questions asked rather than repeating their knowledge without application.

Candidates should note that from the April 2025 exam session, all examinations will
continue to be delivered virtually and will have online proctoring. Exams will be closed
book and closed web. The ability to refer to past examiner reports and past papers
during the exam is not permitted. Candidates attempting to do so will be in breach of
the Assessment Regulations and subject to inappropriate conduct
investigations. Further details of the new exams can be found on the IFOA website.


The report is written based on the legislative and regulatory context pertaining to the date that
the examination was set. Candidates should take into account the possibility that
circumstances may have changed if using these reports for revision.

A.     General comments on the aims of this subject and how it is marked


The aim of this subject is to ensure that the successful candidate can analyse data, develop a
model, and document the work (including maintaining an audit trail for a fellow student and
senior actuary). They should be able to analyse the methods used and outputs generated and
communicate to a senior actuary the approach, results and conclusions.

The subject is split into two papers. The first paper, dealt with in this report, covers the
objectives:

·      analysis of data.
·      development of a model with clear documentation.

As the focus of the subject is on communication, the majority of the available marks in the
exam are for the documentation and outputs generated rather than for technical modelling
skills. For example, a technical mistake is only penalised once, and candidates can still earn
marks for accurate and clear communication of what was done.

Candidates who give well-reasoned points not in the marking schedule were awarded marks
for doing so.

The Examiners recommend prospective candidates attempt a number of past papers and pay
close attention to both the model solutions and marking schedule to get a better idea of the
approach to analysing data and model documentation that the examiners are looking for.


B.     Comments on candidate performance in this diet of the examination

The overall difficulty of the paper is broadly consistent with that of the average CP2 diet.
This has been reflected in a pass mark of 60% for this exam.

Modelling
The paper had a focus on data validation, ensuring the data used for the modelling was
appropriate and fit for purpose. Data validation was a key differentiator between average and
high-performing candidates. While most candidates performed reasonably well, only the
strongest candidates demonstrated the ability to identify the subtleties necessary for thorough
data validation checks.

Beyond data validation, the modelling was generally answered well by the candidates who
attempted it. There were signs that the less-prepared candidates spent too much time focusing
on the data validation element of the exam and did not leave themselves enough time in the
exam to complete the required modelling, which was not overly complex.

Prospective candidates are reminded that there is an expectation that any element of the
earlier Core Principles subjects (e.g. hypothesis testing) can be drawn on in the CP2 exam. It
is therefore recommended that CP2 is best approached after being familiar with the content of
these earlier subjects.

Audit trail
Most candidates did well in the audit trail. The description of methodology, with emphasis on
both what was done and how each step was performed, was well handled by the better
prepared candidates. Performance in the reasonableness checks section has improved. Most
candidates managed to propose a few sensible and relevant reasonableness checks and
comments.

There were some candidate’s audit trails where it was clear, the candidate had run out of
time. The examiners would like to remind prospective candidates that this exam primarily
tests communication, so it is important for candidates to ensure they have allocated sufficient
time during the exam to produce a complete audit trail alongside the modelling work that is
required.

 Commentary
 The modelling for this paper was data focused with the actual modelling being reasonably
 simple throughout albeit with enough complexity to allow better candidates the opportunity to
 display a more thorough understanding of the assignment.

 Data analysis was generally well handled by most candidates. The errors in the data were
 found and a variety of corrections were made, which were all acceptable provided a rationale
 was given.

 On the journey duration calculation, many candidates simply subtracted the start time from
 the end time, resulting in some negative journey duration. This should ring alarm bells and
 prompt candidates to revisit the calculation. Instead, candidates blindly followed their
 calculation resulting in valid data being adjusted or removed.

 Only the stronger candidates were able to examine the nuances in the data. In addition to the
 correct journey duration calculations, these candidates also checked the data provided was
 indeed taken in July and the taxi demand did fall when fare increased. These subtleties were
 missed by less prepared candidates.

 Hypothesis testing, despite having been featured in many past exams, threw some candidates
 off. The marking schedule above covers the chi-squared test, but full marks were awarded for
 any alternative approach, including the use of excel in-built functionalities.

 Better candidates managed to complete the modelling and calculate the expected new fare and
 the adjustment required to reduce taxi demand correctly in the alternative scenario. Less well-
 prepared candidates tended to struggle with the data validation section and did not progress
 to the actual modelling stage, which was designed to be relatively straightforward. The
 examiners advise prospective candidates to work on their exam techniques and be disciplined
 with their time in the exam, so they do not invest a disproportionate amount of time on a
 particular question or section.

 Candidate’s charts were generally well constructed with axes labelled clearly.

 Commentary
 In general, candidate’s audit trails were fairly well written. With better candidates showing
 evidence of effectively planning their time during the exam, and were therefore able to focus
 their attention on the key areas of the modelling and audit trail.
 Most candidates struggled with providing assumptions that add value to the audit trail.
 Reinstating information provided in the background information or listing items not assumed
 to change are not assumptions that add value. The quality of the assumptions matters. For
 example simply repeating that the journey distance should be at least 0.1 miles with a
 maximum of 100 miles does not add value, instead interpreting the instructions and their
 impact on the modelling does, such as ‘July data is representative of the City’s taxi usage, i.e.
 there are no holidays or seasonality effect which might skew the data’.
 The methodology section was generally well written and at the level appropriate to both a
 fellow student and a senior actuary. Stronger candidates were able to note what has been
 done as well as how it was done in the model.
 Reasonableness checks, whilst still far from ideal, have improved significantly in this diet,
 with most candidates giving some relevant comments on the reasonableness of the charts
 plotted. For example, we would expect taxi demand to drop as the weekend closes which is in
 line with the shape of the chart, or noting how the demand varies over the course of a day,
 broadly in line with traditional working hours.
