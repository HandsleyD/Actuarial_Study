Background
Your local university has received a donation of $250,000 from a local businessperson. The
businessperson has asked that the donated money be used to provide a scholarship to selected
students who are completing their actuarial degree at the university.

The actuarial degree lasts for 3 years. Under the scholarship, every year two students going
into their third year will each receive a $10,000 payment to cover the costs of the final year of
their actuarial degree. The two students chosen to receive the scholarship will have achieved
the highest average marks in their second year. The first scholarship payments will be made
at the beginning of the 2024 academic year, i.e. on 1 September 2024 and annually thereafter.
The payments will be made as lump sums on this date. Payments will cease when there is not
enough money to make the scholarship payments in a particular year. When this occurs, any
undistributed money in the fund will be returned to the university.

The university will invest the donation from the businessperson in a suitable investment fund
until it is distributed to the students. The university has chosen a passive equity fund to be the
investment fund, which is expected to achieve annual returns in line with the All Share Index
(ASI) total returns.

The university has asked your actuarial consultancy firm to complete some modelling about
the scholarship. You have joined the team that will carry out this modelling. The modelling
(the core projection) required is to determine:

   How many years it should be possible to pay the scholarship payments to students.
   The money weighted rate of return on the money donated that will be achieved over this
    period.

Your colleague has carried out some initial investigations and has analysed the historic
returns of the ASI (total return) between 2003 and 2022. Your colleague has obtained the
historic ASI values over this period from the internet. They have completed regression
analysis on the logarithm with base 10 of the index values at each date over this period and
have determined that future values of the logarithm with base 10 of this index could be
determined from a linear equation.

The university is also aware that there will be some volatility in the future equity returns.
They have therefore asked your firm to complete ten simulations to allow for the volatility in
the fund projections. For these simulations, your firm has generated 250 random numbers
from the Uniform (0,1) distribution and these have been used to determine residuals that
could be added to the linear equation. The residuals are assumed to be normally distributed.
The mean and variance of this distribution have been determined from an analysis of the
residuals from the regression analysis.

Your colleague has completed the calculation to project the fund over a period of 25 years
(for both the core projection and the simulations).

The results of these calculations need to be presented in a report to the university. However,
your colleague is now out of the office on leave and your manager has asked you to prepare
this report based on the work already completed by your colleague.

Audit Trail – Actuarial Scholarship Fund Analysis
Objective

The local university has received a donation from a local businessperson. The university will
invest this donation in a fund, from which it will pay two students a scholarship each year
while sufficient money remains in the fund. The university wishes to invest the donation in a
passive equity fund until the money is distributed to students.

The model completes fund projections over 25 years to determine the period over which the
scholarship could be paid, the money weighted rate of return on the donation, and some
scenario analysis work using simulations to illustrate the volatility of investment returns.

Data

The local university has advised that the donation received from the local businessperson is
$250,000 and that the scholarship payment to each student will be $10,000. Two payments
will be made to the top performing students from the second year of the university course.
The first payment will be made on 1 September 2024, and annually thereafter and will be
made as a one-off lump sum payment.

The historic index values of the ASI (total return) has been obtained from the internet.

Data checks

The following data checks have been completed on the ASI data:

    The number of date entries is calculated in cell A5 using the COUNT function.
    Column B calculates the difference in days between subsequent date entries. Cells B2 and
     B3 calculate the minimum and maximum of the results from these calculations using the
     MIN and MAX function respectively.
    Column D checks that each index value is greater than zero. The output is determined
     using an IF statement. Cells D2 and D3 calculate the number of outputs that are ‘OK’ and
     the number of outputs that produce an ‘ERROR’.
    Column F calculates the percentage increase between subsequent entries by taking the
     index value at t + 1 and dividing by the index value at t. Cells F2 and F3 calculate the
     minimum and maximum of the results from these calculations using the MIN and MAX
     function respectively.

Assumptions

1.   It is assumed that the donation will be invested on 1 September 2024.
2.   It is possible to make withdrawals from the fund to make the scholarship payments.
3.   The daily observations are independent.
4.   The data obtained from the internet is correct.

Regression analysis

This sheet includes the output from the regression analysis.

The results from Excel’s regression analysis are output into the ‘Regression analysis’

The residuals for each date are included in cells A24 to C5075. A check has been added in

The level of R-square of about 0.94 confirms that about 94% of the variability is explained by

Parameters

This worksheet includes the parameters required for the fund projection.

The linear regression equation has been summarised in row 3 based on the outputs from the

Equity – core

This worksheet completes the fund projection and calculates the number of years the
scholarship payment can be made and calculates the money weighted investment return.

Column A shows the year of projection from year 1 to year 25 and column B shows the
starting date for that year of projection. The first date in cell B3 is brought in from the
parameters tab. The remaining dates are calculated using the EDATE function, based on the
opening date (cell B3) and (year for that row less one) multiplied by twelve.

The projected log ASI value at each date (Log ASIdate) is then calculated in column C. This is
calculated using the linear regression formula:

                         Log ASIdate = Intercept + date × X Variable1

The logarithm with base 10 has been used and the intercept and the X Variable1 values are
obtained from the ‘Parameters’ worksheet.

Column D calculates the projected ASI value at each date by calculating 10 to the power of
the result from column C.

The fund at the beginning of each year is shown in column E. The entry in cell E3 is the
donation amount. Subsequent entries are equal to the End of Year (EOY) fund from the
previous year in column H.

Column F calculates the expected scholarships to be paid at each date by multiplying the
individual scholarship amount by the number of scholarships awarded each year.

An indicator is then shown in column G to determine if the fund at the beginning of the year
is greater than the expected scholarships payment at that date. Where the fund is greater, the
output is Yes, otherwise the output is No.

The EOY fund is calculated in column H. If the fund at the beginning of the year is greater
than the expected scholarship payment for that year, the EOY fund is calculated as:

                                                                              ASI at EOYt
  Fund value at EOYt        Fund value at EOYt-1      Scholarships paid
                                                                              ASI at EOYt-1

Otherwise, it is assumed that there is insufficient money in the fund to pay scholarships in
that year and therefore the fund is returned to the university at the beginning of the year and
the fund reduces to nil.

The relevant statistics are then calculated in cells K2 to O10.

The number of years for which scholarship payments will be made is calculated in cell O2.
This is calculated using the COUNTIF on column G where we count the number of ‘Yes’
entries.

The residual assets, after the last possible scholarship payment has been made, is then
calculated in cell O4. VLOOKUP is used to determine the fund at the beginning of the year,
based on the number of years of payments calculated above plus 1 year.

The money weighted investment return is then shown in cell O6. This cell is required for the
goal seek (see below). An arbitrary entry is initially included in this cell.

Cell O8 calculates the present value of the scholarships paid plus the residual assets. A
temporary annuity is calculated using the formula:
                                                                  term
                                           1     1 MWRR
                            temp ann
                                                  MWRR
                                                 1 MWRR

where MWRR is the Money Weighted Rate of Return and term is the number of years for
which scholarship payments will be made.

The present value is calculated as the scholarship payment × number of annual scholarships
paid × temp ann + residual assets × (1 + MWRR)−term.

This worksheet includes random numbers generated from the U(0,1) distribution and converts

This sheet completes the simulations of the fund projection allowing for a residual variable

This worksheet is a copy of the ‘Equity – core’ worksheet but a new column has been added
in column C to pick up a generated residual from the ‘Random #’ worksheet for each year
and each simulation. Column D calculates the projected log ASI using the formula:

                  Log ASIdate = Intercept + date × X Variable1+ residualdate

The remaining calculations are the same as the ‘Equity – core’ worksheet. The same Goal

can be funded (the fund value at the end of the year was used to distinguish between
simulations with an equal number of funded years).
