CP22 A2025–3
Background
You are a student actuary in an actuarial consultancy.

Our client, a cinema, is considering offering a new ticket pricing option and has approached
your company to assess the financial impact of introducing the new option.

To do this, your company is going to perform a monthly profit analysis for a 1-year period.

To support this, the client has provided the following details:

   The cinema classifies movies into three categories:
    o Epic (one release per year)
    o Major (three releases per year)
    o Regular (ten releases per year).

    They have shared the movie schedule of a typical year.

   The cinema has four screens with different seating capacities. They have shared with you
    the expected screen utilisation rates. Each screen has six showings per day.

Currently, the cinema revenue comes from two sources (‘original scenario’): ticket sales and
snack purchases.

   Cinema tickets cost $13 per ticket.
   When snacks are purchased, the average snack spend is $22.
   The average cost per snack is $2.
   The proportion of cinemagoers who purchase snacks varies based on the number of
    movies seen in a year.
   Given some people may go to the cinema many times a year, the client also provides
    information on the number of unique cinemagoers and their snack spending habits.

Expenses:

   The cinema has two teams with each team having 16 staff. Staff work 8-hour shifts, with
    each staff member paid an hourly rate of $25.
   The cinema shows movies from movie distributors at no upfront cost to the cinema. The
    cinema pays the movie distributors a commission, i.e. a percentage of the ticket price. The
    cinema enters a commission arrangement with movie distributors based on the attendance
    and how long a movie has been in the cinema. The details have been shared with your
    colleague.
   The cinema incurs a fixed overheads cost of $300,000 a month.

The client is looking to offer cinemagoers an annual unlimited movie pass (an ‘annual pass’).
For the annual pass option, instead of buying tickets for each movie individually,
cinemagoers can watch as many movies as they like during the year for a fixed annual pass
fee payable at the start of each year.

The client has undertaken market research to understand what features cinemagoers would
like the annual pass option to have.


CP22 A2025–4
The market research has indicated the following:

   An annual pass fee of $74 would be acceptable.
   The cinema should offer a 10% discount on all snack purchases to cinemagoers who have
    bought the annual pass to reward their loyalty.
   Cinemagoers who watch at least five movies a year would all choose to purchase an
    annual pass.
   Experience data from other cinemas shows that cinemagoers who buy the annual pass
    would typically spend 20% more on snacks due to the perceived value of the annual pass.

The commission arrangement would be unaffected by the introduction of an annual pass
option. The movie distributor’s commission would still be calculated based on a percentage
of the individual ticket price, irrespective of whether the cinemagoer has purchased an annual
pass or they have purchased tickets individually.

Based on the information provided by the client and the market research, your colleague has
calculated the profit for both the original and annual pass option scenarios.

Additional considerations

Your manager has verbally communicated the results of the initial analysis to the client. The
client would additionally like you to calculate an adjusted ticket price and annual pass fee for
a new scenario where:

   all cinemagoers who watch at least four movies a year would all purchase an annual pass.
   the profit would be the same as the annual pass scenario above, where cinemagoers who
    watch at least five movies a year would all purchase the pass.
   the annual pass fee to ticket price ratio would be 4.8.

Your colleague is out on sabbatical, so your manager has asked you to prepare this report
based on the work already completed by your colleague.

For this additional scenario, you can assume:

   there is no change in demand from cinemagoers because of any price changes.
   cinemagoers who watch at least four movies a year would purchase an annual pass and
    these cinemagoers would also spend 20% more on snacks given a 10% discount.

Your manager has asked you to prepare a summary covering all elements of the work (both
the original work that your colleague completed and the additional modelling that you have
completed).

You are not expected to include the additional spreadsheet work you undertake in the
audit trail, but all the methodology and all results should be included in the summary.

Objective

Our client, a cinema, is considering introducing a new ticket pricing option for cinemagoers.
The cinema currently sells individual tickets and snacks, and the cinema management would
like to understand the financial impact of offering an unlimited movie annual pass option.

The purpose of the model is to estimate the expected revenue, costs and profits over a 1-year
period and to assess the financial impact if an annual pass is introduced.

Data

The client has provided the following details:

   Movie schedule and seating capacity for the four screens.
   Movies are classified into three categories: Epic, Major and Regular releases.
   Projected screen utilisation rate.
   Commission arrangement (percentage of ticket price) with the movie distributors.
   Unique attendance details split by the number of movies seen over a year and snack
    purchasing habit.
   Each screen runs six showings per day with a ticket price of $13.
   The average snack spend is $22 and the average snack cost is $2.
   The cinema has two teams of staff with each team having 16 staff working an 8-hour shift
    at an hourly rate of $25.
   There is a monthly overhead of $300,000.

Separately the market research undertaken by the client indicates:

   an annual pass fee of $74 would be acceptable to cinemagoers.
   cinemagoers who watch five or more movies a year would purchase the pass without
    exception.
   a 10% discount on snacks should be offered to annual passholders.
   annual passholders would typically spend 20% more on snacks if they purchase an
    annual pass.

Data checks

The ‘Data_checks’ worksheet performs checks on the data.

The number of annual showings by movie is calculated in C13:C27 by using a COUNTIF
function. The data suggests the Epic release is shown the most times, followed by Major
releases, which seems to make sense. Each movie is shown for 2–4 months over a year. This
does not appear unreasonable.

Additional guidance
COUNTIF

The COUNTIF function counts the number of cells that meet a specific criteria.

COUNTIF(range, criteria)

Example: Count cells in A1 to A4 if they are greater than 100:

=COUNTIF(A1:A4,">100")

INDEX

The INDEX function returns the value of an element in a table (or array), for the given row
and column numbers.

INDEX(array, row_num, [column_num])

   array: A range of cells (i.e. table)
   row_num: Selects the row in the table from which to return a value
   column_num: Selects the column in the table from which to return a value.

MATCH

The MATCH function searches for a specified item in a range of cells and returns the relative
position of that item in the range.

MATCH(lookup_value, lookup_array, [match_type])

   lookup_value: The value to match in the lookup_array
   lookup_array: The range of cells being searched
   match_type (optional): The number −1, 0 or 1, specifying how Excel matches the
    lookup_value with values in the lookup_array. Default value is 1.

SUMIF

The SUMIF function adds a range of values that meet specific criteria.

SUMIF(range, criteria, [sum_range])

Range is the list of values to sum, criteria is the value of the cells to be added and sum_range
(optional) is a list of cells to actually sum if not the range.

Example: Total values in B1 to B4 if text starting with a Y is in column A:

=SUMIF(A1:A4,"Y*",B1:B4)
