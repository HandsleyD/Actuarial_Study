     Prepare an audit trail for your spreadsheet model that includes the following aspects:

           purpose of the model
           data, including checks, the results of the checks and any action taken to change the
            data
           assumptions used
           methodology, i.e. description of how each calculation stage in the model has been
            produced
           explanation of any further checks performed.

     You should ensure that your audit trail is suitable for both a senior actuary who has
     been asked to approve your work and a fellow student who has been asked to peer
     review and correct your model or may be asked to continue to work on it or to use it

CP21 A2025–4
Background
The government of a developing country is concerned about increased levels of pollution in
the country. The government has undertaken research and found that the most significant
contributor to pollution is the high number of petrol cars in the country.

As a result, the government has established a Clean Transport (CT) programme with the
objective of promoting the sale of electric cars, encouraging citizens to switch from petrol
cars to electric cars over the next 20 years.

To do this, the government proposes to:

1. offer a fixed subsidy of $3,000 on the price of every electric car purchased in the country.

2. build charging stations across the country to meet the demand of electric car users
   recharging their car batteries.

The government has agreed a budget of $3 billion as of 1 January 2026 to launch and
implement this programme. They have asked the government finance department to project
the expected yearly cashflows from the programme over the following 20 years to assess if
the current budget will be sufficient. They have called this the ‘base scenario’.

Modelling

You work in the government finance department. Your manager has asked you to project
the expected number of petrol cars and electric cars that will run in the country over the next
20 years, assuming the CT programme is implemented.

For this purpose, the government’s transport department has provided the following data
items:

   The number of petrol cars in the country as of 1 January 2026 is 5,000,000.
   The expected monthly sales of new petrol cars from 2026 to 2045, before accounting for
    the launch of the CT programme, is provided in the data file.

You are required to carry out checks on the sales data provided by the transport department,
plot a suitable chart of this data to identify any errors and make appropriate amendments
before using it for any calculations.

Base scenario

It is estimated that, were the CT programme implemented, 5% of the expected sales of new
petrol cars for the year 2026 will be replaced by the sale of new electric cars. For each future
year, this proportion will increase by 1% p.a. (e.g. 5% in 2026, 6% in 2027, 7% in 2028, etc.).

At the end of each year, 4% of the petrol cars in the country will be scrapped. For electric
cars, no cars will be scrapped over the next 15 years (i.e. until the end of 2040) but thereafter
a scrap rate of 3% p.a. will apply.

Based on the corrected data and allowing for the CT programme launch, you should project:

   the yearly sales of electric and petrol cars over each of the next 20 years.
   the expected total number of petrol and electric cars in the country at the end of each year.

As a next step, your manager would like you to:

   project forward the expected yearly cashflows for the CT programme over the next 20
    years starting from 1 January 2026.
   calculate the NPV of these cashflows.
   assess how the NPV compares to the current budget as at 1 January 2026.

To help you with this modelling, the analytics team in the government finance department
has investigated and set out the following underlying parameters to be used for the
projections:

   Discount rate of 7% p.a.
   The energy requirement for a full recharge of the battery of one electric car is estimated
    to be 50 kWh and each car will need a full recharge 90 times per year.
   It has been estimated that each charging station will be able to supply 438,000 kWh of
    energy in a year for charging batteries of electric cars. The cost of building one such
    charging station is estimated to be $30,000 in 2026, increasing with inflation of 3% p.a.
    thereafter. It is assumed that enough charging stations will be built to meet the demand.
   Programme expenses will include:
    o administration costs of $150,000 p.a.
    o advertising and other costs of $50,000 p.a.
    These costs will rise with inflation of 3% p.a. after 2026.

Additional scenario

To mitigate some of the additional costs from the CT programme, the government is
considering introducing an additional tax on petrol prices from 1 January 2026. The
additional tax rate will be 10% on the price of petrol. The government plans to use this
additional tax income to meet the costs of the CT programme, in addition to the agreed
budget.

Your manager has asked you to perform a further projection of yearly cashflows for the
additional scenario and complete the same analysis as the base scenario.

For this purpose, the department has provided the price of 1 litre of petrol to be $1.20 in
2026, inflating at 1.5% p.a. thereafter. It has been estimated that the average consumption of
petrol per car in a year is 300 litres.
