1    An Accumulating With-Profits (AWP) policy provides a benefit of an accumulating
     fund of premiums.

     A life insurance company issues a 30-year AWP endowment assurance policy to lives
     aged 33 exact. Premiums of £4,000 p.a. are payable annually in advance for the first
     25 years while the policy is in force.

     The death benefit, payable at the end of the year of death, is the greater of £100,000
     and the value of the AWP fund. On surrender the policyholder receives a refund of the
     total premiums paid, without interest. The maturity benefit is equal to the value of the
     AWP fund at the end of the term.

     All policy terms and profit test assumptions are set out on the ‘Q1 Base’ worksheet.

     (i)    Calculate the surrender and death benefits payable for each year of the policy,
            and the maturity benefit payable on survival to the end of the term.         [8]

     (ii)   Calculate the profit vector for this policy.                                  [11]

2    The worksheet ‘Q2 Base’ gives details of a company’s liability cashflows over the
     next 30 years.

     (i)    Calculate the present value and discounted mean term of the liabilities at an
            interest rate of 2% p.a. effective.                                           [7]

     The company buys a 20-year zero-coupon bond redeemable at 200%, which has an
     annual effective yield equal to 2% p.a.

     (ii)   (a)     Calculate the nominal amount of this bond that the company should
                    purchase in order to hold assets that would have the same present value
                    as its liabilities.                                                 [3]

            (b)     Determine if the company’s liabilities will be immunised against small
                    changes in interest rates, at the interest rate used in part (ii), if this
                    amount of the bond is held.

                    Do not perform any further calculations but your answer should
                    include a discussion of each of Redington’s three conditions.        [8]
                                                                                  [Total 18]

3    A life insurance company issues a portfolio of special assurance policies designed to
     provide family income protection in the event of death or critical illness.

     The policies have a term of 30 years and are issued to a portfolio of lives currently
     aged 38 exact. In the event of a policyholder’s death or on diagnosis of a critical
     illness, an annuity certain of $1,000 per month is paid to the policyholder’s family for
     the remainder of the 30-year term.

     Level premiums are payable annually in advance, ceasing on the payment of any
     benefit.

     Withdrawals are allowed at the end of each policy year and after withdrawal, the
     policy terminates immediately.

     The worksheet ‘Q3 Base’ sets out the terms of the policy as well as the basis used to
     price it, including the independent decrement rates.

     (i)     Calculate the dependent decrement rates for each year of the policy for this
             portfolio.                                                                   [8]

     (ii)    Calculate the premium charged for each policy in the portfolio.             [20]

     (iii)   (a)    Explain why having level premiums for this policy is a risk to the
                    company.

             (b)    Discuss possible changes that the company could make to mitigate this
                    risk.
                                                                                      [4]
                                                                              [Total 32]

4    Investor X purchased $1 million nominal of a newly issued 20-year index-linked bond
     on 31 December 2014 at a price of $80 per $100 nominal.

     The bond pays half-yearly coupons at a rate of 2% p.a. and a final redemption
     payment of $100 per $100 nominal, all of which are increased in line with an inflation
     index allowing for a 2-month time lag.

     Worksheet ‘Q4 Base’ sets out the historic inflation index and future assumptions for
     inflation and tax (income and capital gains). Income tax is payable immediately, i.e. at
     the time the coupons are paid. Capital gains tax is payable at the date of redemption.

     Ten years after issue, immediately after the coupon payment is made, Investor X sells
     the bond to Investor A. Investor A is a taxpayer.

     (i)    Calculate the theoretical price Investor A should pay to achieve a net
            effective annual money yield of 8% p.a., assuming they hold the bond to
            maturity.                                                               [20]

     The actual price Investor A pays for the bond holding is $1,440,000. Investor X does
     not pay any tax.

     (ii)   Show that the annual real yield obtained by Investor X is approximately
            3.25% p.a.                                                                  [11]

     You should assume that all months are of equal length.
                                                                                  [Total 31]
