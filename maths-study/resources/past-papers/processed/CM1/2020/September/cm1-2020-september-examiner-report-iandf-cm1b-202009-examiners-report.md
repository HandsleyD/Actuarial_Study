Introduction


The Examiners’ Report is written by the Chief Examiner with the aim of helping candidates,
both those who are sitting the examination for the first time and using past papers as a
revision aid and also those who have previously failed the subject.

The Examiners are charged by Council with examining the published syllabus. The
Examiners have access to the Core Reading, which is designed to interpret the syllabus, and
will generally base questions around it but are not required to examine the content of Core
Reading specifically or exclusively.

For numerical questions the Examiners’ preferred approach to the solution is reproduced in
this report; other valid approaches are given appropriate credit. For essay-style questions,
particularly the open-ended questions in the later subjects, the report may contain more points
than the Examiners will expect from a solution that scores full marks.

The report is written based on the legislative and regulatory context pertaining to the date that
the examination was set. Candidates should take into account the possibility that
circumstances may have changed if using these reports for revision.

     A. General comments on the aims of this subject and how it is marked

1. CM1 provides a grounding in the principles of modelling as applied to actuarial work –
   focusing particularly on deterministic models which can be used to model and value
   known cashflows as well as those which are dependent on death, survival, or other
   uncertain risks.
2. The worksheets provided to candidates for each question give a suggested format for part
   or all of the solution and the methodology used in the model solutions follow these
   suggested formats. Candidates are not penalised for using a valid alternative approach.
   The worksheets are also designed so that the final numerical answers for some questions
   are to be shown on a specified ‘Answers’ sheet. Candidates are not penalised for not
   using these sheets if their final answers are clearly shown on their working sheets.
3. Candidates may lose marks where insufficient working is shown.
4. The exam is not designed to be a test of Excel skills. Thus, some functionality which may
   be preferred in a real-world work environment is not necessarily required to answer the
   questions. However, some good practices are useful to the candidate e.g. including
   variables/parameters the question states will change as inputs rather than hard coding
   these into formulae.

Q1

 In part a), candidates seemed unfamiliar with the recursive approach for calculating
 reserves.

 Many candidates who did correctly identify the individual cashflow elements of the
 reserve lost marks through discounting back from the end of the policy instead of rolling
 up from the start of the policy. This showed a lack of understanding of the required
 methodology.

 Other common errors included
 • incorrectly applying the survivorship adjustment to the end-year cashflow/reserve
   figure to take it to the start of the following year
 • including a renewal expense for year 1
 • including a maturity outgo.

 Around two-thirds of candidates failed to score on part b).



Q2


The majority of candidates obtained full marks in part (i).

In part (ii), many candidates struggled to correctly incorporate the increases in coupon
and redemption payments over time due to the application of the inflation index. This
seemed similar to the problems candidates have had with this topic when similar
questions have been asked in past CT1 exams. Some of the candidates who recognised
the need to increase the payments, had difficulty in determining which months’ index
values to use in their calculations.

The majority of candidates failed to score any marks in part (iii). It is important to
recognise that whilst this exam primarily tests calculation skills, candidates will be
required to analyse and comment on their results.

Q3


Candidates seemed to be able to calculate answers for columns where explicit headings
were given (e.g. qx death, mu x death, qx surrender and mux surrender in part (i)) but then
did not recognise the need to use them to calculate values later in the question (e.g. for
(aq)x death and surrender rates in part (ii)).

Many candidates missed the either the probability of surviving to the start of the policy
year or discounting for interest when calculating the PV of benefits and premiums.
Many candidates also failed to include an annuity cost when calculating the premium.

In part (iii), most candidates missed the fact that the early retirement pension would be of
a reduced amount, but credit was given for any relevant points. The marking schedule for
part (iii) includes possible calculations that a candidate could have made as part of their
answer. Such calculations were not necessarily expected, and full marks could be
obtained without these.
