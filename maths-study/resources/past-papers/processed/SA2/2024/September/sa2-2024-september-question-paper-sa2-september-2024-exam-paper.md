1    A life insurance company, ABC, has recently entered the whole of life assurance
     market and has launched a new product with simple underwriting to attract
     individuals aged over 50, through direct marketing in the press (newspapers and
     television). The death benefit is fixed at outset, with no options on the policy.
     Policyholders can surrender in the first 3 years of the contract where they will receive
     a return of premiums paid.

     Sales of ABC’s new product have increased rapidly recently, and ABC’s finance team
     has projected the reserves for the product over the next 10 years allowing for expected
     new business in each future year. The projection shows that reserves are negative in
     the early years, then positive in future periods.

     (i)      Explain why the reserves may initially be negative for this product.          [4]

     ABC is based in a country where the local reporting regime requires negative reserves
     to be restricted to zero. ABC’s management is considering options to release the value
     of the negative reserves to improve the balance sheet position.

     (ii)     Suggest possible reasons why ABC would want to release the value of the
              negative reserves.                                                      [4]

     ABC is discussing an arrangement with an external reinsurer where the reinsurer
     would pay ABC a lump sum in exchange for a fixed proportion of ABC’s future
     profits on whole of life business written to date.

     (iii)    Set out the considerations for the reinsurer when determining the lump sum
              payment to offer ABC.                                                     [10]

     (iv)     Discuss the risks of entering this arrangement from the perspective of ABC
              and the reinsurer.                                                         [4]

     ABC sets its maintenance expense assumption for reserves at a product level based on
     both its direct and indirect costs, with indirect costs allocated based on the current
     number of policies in force. Due to an anticipated rapid increase in future sales,
     ABC’s reserving actuary is proposing to allocate the indirect costs on the expected
     number of policies in 5 years’ time.

     (v)      Discuss the reasons for and against this proposal.                            [5]

2    A life insurance company, Company A, sold conventional with-profits endowment
     assurances and conventional with-profits group personal pensions for many years, but
     closed these products to new business a few years ago.

     (i)      Explain how these products would be accounted for, following the
              introduction of International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) 17.       [3]

     (ii)     Describe the transitional arrangements that Company A may have adopted for
              these products when introducing IFRS 17.                                [5]

     Company A has developed a unit-linked endowment assurance to replace the
     conventional with-profits endowment assurance.

     (iii)    Describe, for each of these two products, how best estimate liabilities would
              be determined under Solvency II.                                             [6]

     Over the last financial year, Company A has seen a large fall in the Solvency II best
     estimate liabilities for the unit-linked endowment.

     (iv)     Suggest possible reasons for this fall.                                    [11]

     Company A is considering selling its portfolio of conventional with-profits
     endowment assurances to another life insurance company, Company B. This portfolio
     is not administered by Company A, but by an external outsourcer.

     (v)      Discuss the issues that Company A would need to consider before proceeding
              with the sale.                                                          [12]

3    A life insurance company operates in a single country. The company writes regular
     premium level term assurance and decreasing term assurance business with both types
     of policy having the option to add critical illness cover. The company has decided to
     expand its range of protection products. To do so it wants to understand its potential
     customers’ needs.

     (i)       Outline the ways in which the company could determine customer needs.         [4]

     (ii)      Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each of the level and decreasing
               term business from the perspective of the policyholder.                    [5]

     The company has decided to launch an increasing term assurance product with the
     following features:

           Premiums will be discounted by a fixed percentage for policyholders who can
            demonstrate that they are following a healthy lifestyle.
           Each year the policyholder will have the option to increase the death benefit in
            line with a defined index. No underwriting will occur in respect of these increases.
            Premiums for the increase will be based on the then current new business
            premium rates and, if applicable, the heathy lifestyle reductions in force at the
            time.

     As part of the product design process, the company is considering its overall approach
     to underwriting.

     (iii)     Describe how the general business environment could impact the company’s
               underwriting approach at the point of new business.                    [4]

     (iv)      Discuss the impact that this new product will have on the risks that the
               company faces.                                                               [15]

     (v)       Set out how the company could determine the best estimate mortality
               assumptions for this product.                                                 [4]

     The company is proposing to outsource the tracking of evidence of policyholders’
     healthy lifestyles to an outsource provider based overseas.

     (vi)      Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this proposal.                    [4]
                                                                                      [Total 36]
