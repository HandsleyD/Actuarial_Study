SA2 S2025                                                              © Institute and Faculty of Actuaries
1    A life insurance company (Company A) has taken over several smaller life insurance
     companies in recent years. Company A is now responsible for the management of
     many different products. Some of these products are administered on Company A’s
     internal administration systems, but the administration of some products is carried out
     by several external administrators, covered by outsource agreements. Also, because of
     the takeovers, Company A has three with-profits funds, each from a different legacy
     company.

     Company A is proposing to simplify its administration arrangements by moving all of
     the products onto a smaller number of administration systems.

     (i)      Discuss the issues Company A would need to consider in relation to this
              proposal.                                                                 [20]

     Company A is also proposing to simplify the future terms and conditions for in-force
     business, making the products easier to understand. For example, one proposal is to
     change the existing charging structure for unit-linked products, which includes a
     variety of charges, to a simple annual management charge that is more in line with the
     current market.

     (ii)     Discuss the considerations for Company A when deciding what
              simplifications may be made.                                              [10]

     Company A had considered merging the three with-profits funds into one but has
     decided not to do this.

     (iii)    Explain the possible reasons for Company A’s decision not to merge the three
              with-profits funds.                                                       [6]

2    A proprietary life insurance company has only ever sold income protection and
     unit-linked savings products. The unit-linked products give a minimum guarantee
     on death and apply an annual management charge to the funds.

     (i)      Describe the key risks the company is exposed to with respect to the income
              protection business.                                                       [6]

     The company calculates its regulatory capital requirements under Solvency II using
     an internal model. The company’s economic capital requirements are lower than its
     regulatory capital requirements.

     (ii)     Discuss why the company’s economic capital requirements are lower than its
              regulatory capital requirements.                                         [8]

     The company is considering the potential impact of climate change on its business.

     (iii)    Discuss how climate change could impact the key risks faced by the company.
                                                                                      [10]

     (iv)     Discuss how the company could mitigate the potential climate risks it is
              exposed to.                                                                   [6]

     The regulator has asked the company to carry out scenario testing to assess the
     potential impact of climate change on the company’s projected solvency.

     (v)      Justify why scenario testing may be more appropriate than stochastic
              modelling to assess the potential impact of climate change.                   [4]

3    A life insurance company writes non-profit annuities, non-profit term assurances and
     unit-linked endowment assurance products with a death benefit. The company has
     recently completed the 31 December 2024 (YE24) Solvency II balance sheet and
     International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) accounts. All the products are
     treated as insurance contracts and cannot be unbundled.

     (i)      State, with reasons for each product, which IFRS 17 measurement model is
              most likely to be used.                                                  [3]

     The same cashflow model and policy data are used to produce the Best Estimate
     Liabilities (BEL) for Solvency II and for IFRS 17 (also known as the present value of
     future cashflows under IFRS 17). Best estimate assumptions are used for both
     reporting regimes, with the only two differences in the assumptions being the
     maintenance expense and Risk Discount Rate (RDR). The company has produced a
     reconciliation between the Solvency II BEL and the IFRS 17 BEL as a check on the
     results. The reconciliation for the non-profit products is shown in the table below:

                                                        Annuities    Term assurance
       YE24
                                                       ($ million)     ($ million)
       Solvency II BEL                                    1,000           500
                         Expenses                          (50)           (40)
       Move to IFRS      Demographic assumptions            –               –
       assumptions       RDR                               (30)           (50)
                         Inflation                          –              –
       IFRS 17 BEL                                         920            410

     (ii)     Suggest possible reasons for the differences between the Solvency II BEL and
              IFRS 17 BEL at YE24 for the two product types.                            [7]

     Since the previous year end (YE23) the company has revised the best estimate
     assumptions for mortality and for mortality improvements.

     (iii)    Discuss how these changes in experience and assumptions will impact the
              elements of the actuarial results under Solvency II and IFRS 17, respectively.
                                                                                          [8]

     [Note: Candidates are not expected to consider the directional element of the
     impacts.]

     Due to time constraints, the company is planning to produce the 30 June 2025 (HY25)
     results for IFRS 17 using a simplified approach whereby a full actuarial model
     calculation will not be performed to derive the IFRS 17 BEL. Instead, the reserves for
     the non-profit products will be derived by projecting the reserves from the YE24
     actuarial model, to HY25. However, the reserving actuary is concerned that this
     approach is an oversimplification and that the results will not be suitably accurate.

     (iv)     Discuss suitable refinements that could be made to the approach to address the
              actuary’s concerns, without running the full model.                         [9]
