1    A parent insurance company with a number of subsidiaries located around the world,
     has suffered large losses due to extreme weather events in some of the countries in
     which it operates.

     The company’s board are considering their options. One director has suggested that
     the subsidiaries in the worst affected countries should be declared insolvent and
     wound up.

     (i)     Outline the risk management process that the global company could have been
             using to manage its risks.                                               [5]

     (ii)    Discuss how the government and the financial regulator in a country where the
             company’s subsidiary is declared insolvent and wound up may react.        [5]

     (iii)   Set out the considerations that the board should consider when discussing the
             director’s proposal.                                                        [5]

     One of the subsidiary companies affected sponsors a large defined benefit pension
     scheme.

     (iv)    Discuss the actions that the pension scheme’s managers may take to protect
             their members’ benefits if the sponsoring company is declared insolvent. [5]

     The company’s board has subsequently decided not to declare the subsidiary company
     insolvent.

     (v)     Suggest possible actions the subsidiary company could take to minimise the
             impact of extreme weather events on their business in future.              [5]

2    A travel company specialises in selling a wide variety of holidays to holiday makers
     in many countries.

     The company is successful and is looking to expand by offering their own cruise
     holidays. It is considering buying its own cruise ships.

     (i)     Suggest possible reasons why the company could be looking to expand in
             this way.                                                              [6]

     (ii)    Outline the risks to the company of running cruise ships.                     [8]

     (iii)   Describe how the company may determine how much to charge for its cruises.
                                                                                    [8]

     (iv)    Suggest possible ways the company could fund this expansion.                  [4]

     (v)     List the benefits the company could offer to its employees.                   [4]

     (vi)    Outline the issues the company may encounter with providing these benefits.
                                                                                       [4]

     The company is considering offering travel insurance to its customers. It is planning
     to use data it holds on its past customers to price its product.

     (vii)   Outline the risks that could be covered under a travel insurance product.     [4]

     (viii) Describe how the company could manage the risks relating to its travel
            insurance product.                                                             [5]

     (ix)    Discuss the suitability of using the company’s data to price its travel insurance
             product.                                                                       [4]

     Alternatively, the company has the opportunity to purchase data from an insurance
     company that used to write travel insurance products for 10 years but stopped writing
     the business 3 years ago.

     (x)     Explain the issues for the company of using the insurance company’s data to
             price the product.                                                         [4]
                                                                                [Total 51]

3    The government of a developed country, Country A, has recently announced a trade
     deal with a neighbouring country, Country B, which will make it easier for insurance
     companies to trade products in each other’s countries. However, each country will
     keep its own regulatory regime.

     Company X is a general insurance company based in Country A and currently offers
     the following products:

           comprehensive vehicle insurance cover
           vehicle breakdown cover where the breakdown occurs in the country of the
            policyholder’s residence.

     As part of its growth strategy, Company X is considering how it can expand into the
     neighbouring country, Country B, over the next 12 months and has asked an actuary
     for advice.

     (i)       Set out the information that the actuary should request from Company X
               before being able to deliver their advice.                                  [4]

     (ii)      Propose four expansion options for Company X.                               [4]

     (iii)     Outline how the actuary may identify the risks and dependencies arising from
               Company X expanding into Country B.                                        [4]

     (iv)      Discuss how the regulatory regime in Country B could impact the success of
               Company X’s growth strategy.                                             [4]

     (v)       Describe how the actuary can use models to assess any additional capital that
               Company X may be required to hold as a result of its expansion.             [4]

     (vi)      Set out how Company X would assess the success of its expansion in
               12 months’ time.                                                         [4]
                                                                                 [Total 24]
