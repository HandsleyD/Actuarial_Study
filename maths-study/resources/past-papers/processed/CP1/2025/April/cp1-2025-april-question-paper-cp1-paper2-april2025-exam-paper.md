1    A country has many museums located in its capital city. Six of these museums are
     very large and popular, with each having over 2 million annual visitors. Of these
     visitors, 50% are from abroad, 25% from the capital city and 25% from other parts of
     the country. The museums are funded by the government. There is no charge to visit
     the museums.

     The country also has museums located in other cities. These are all much smaller with
     maximum annual visitor numbers all under 1 million.

     The government is planning to move one of the six museums out of the capital city.
     Five different alternative cities in the country are being considered for the relocated
     museum.

     (i)     Suggest why the government may be planning to move one of its museums
             from the capital city.                                                [5]

     (ii)    List ten stakeholders who will be affected by the move.                       [5]

     (iii)   Outline how a model could be used to decide which museum to move and
             to which city.                                                       [6]

     An insurance company has been approached to provide a premium quotation for
     insurance relating to moving the objects to the relocated museum.

     (iv)    Describe the risks associated with moving the objects from one museum to
             another.                                                                 [5]

     (v)     Discuss how the insurance company could determine the premium to
             charge for the insurance policy relating to moving the objects between
             museums.                                                                     [10]

     (vi)    Discuss how the insurance company could manage the retained risk if it
             writes this business.                                                         [4]

     (vii)   Describe other advice an actuary could provide to assist with the running of
             the country’s museums.                                                       [6]

     The move has now been completed. The government is considering moving another
     museum out of the capital city.

     (viii) Explain how the actuarial control cycle could be used to evaluate if the move
            has been a success and if a second move is feasible.                         [6]
                                                                                  [Total 47]

2    A developed country has a rapidly ageing population. The state pension dependency
     ratio in the country is defined as the number of citizens older than the State Pension
     Age (SPA) divided by the number of citizens aged between 16 and the SPA.

     Over the next 30 years, this ratio is expected to increase from 0.30 to 0.40.

     The cost of providing social security benefits, including the state pension, to citizens
     in the country is projected to increase significantly over this period.

     Currently the country provides, to all citizens aged over the SPA, a universal state
     pension equal to one third of median average earnings, healthcare and a fixed
     household heating benefit. Citizens may also have income from other sources
     such as company pension benefits, private pensions and income from savings and
     investments. Citizens over the SPA with the lowest overall income may qualify for
     additional social security benefits.

     The state pension currently increases each year by the higher of:

           the annual increase in average earnings
           the annual increase in price inflation
           3%.

     All employed citizens in the country are required to be automatically enrolled into
     their employer’s retirement benefit scheme. This could be either a defined benefit or
     defined contribution retirement benefit scheme. Currently, individuals are permitted
     to opt out of their employer retirement benefit scheme and they are not required to
     make any other retirement provision.

     The government of the country is concerned about the following:

           Intergenerational fairness between citizens above and below the SPA
           Individuals with inadequate retirement savings increasing the burden on the state
            to provide additional social security benefits
           Individuals over the SPA exhausting their retirement savings during their lifetime
           Individuals over the SPA who own their own home having most of their assets in
            property making them ‘asset rich but cash poor’
           Recent increases in the marginal tax rates paid by the highest earners has led to a
            reduction in hours worked, and an increased rate of early retirement and higher
            emigration.

     The government has made an announcement that it is committed to intergenerational
     fairness to ensure that the needs of citizens of each generation are met without
     compromising each other.

     (i)       Propose, with reasons, insurance products that may be suitable for a pensioner
               aged over the SPA.                                                          [4]

     (ii)      Discuss why an employed individual with private healthcare insurance may
               decide to stop renewing their policy after they retire.                  [7]

     (iii)     Outline issues that could arise with a government removing universal state
               social security benefit from citizens of all ages.                         [7]

     The government is proposing to impose a minimum compulsory rate of retirement
     savings for employed and self-employed individuals aged below the SPA, expressed
     as a percentage of income.

     (iv)      Explain how the government’s proposal could affect the cost of social security
               benefits in the future for citizens over the SPA.                          [6]

     (v)       Discuss the potential impact on intergenerational fairness if the government
               significantly increased inheritance taxes.                                   [7]

     The government has made a second proposal to alter the current universal state
     pension by:

           increasing the SPA by 3 years over the next 5 years.
           reducing the minimum rate of increase immediately from 3% down to 2%.
           removing the link to the increase in average earnings.

     (vi)      Discuss the potential impact of this second proposal on:

                    the country’s finances.
                    the government.
                    citizens.
                                                                                              [8]

     In the country, significant taxes are levied each time a citizen purchases a residential
     property. The government is proposing to refund this tax to citizens aged over the
     SPA who sell their existing residential property and replace it with another that costs
     less than 50% of the property sold.

     (vii)     Assess the effectiveness of the government in helping to address the
               government’s concerns about:

               (a)      intergenerational fairness between citizens above and below the SPA.

               (b)      individuals with inadequate retirement savings increasing the burden
                        on the state to provide additional social security benefits.

               (c)      individuals over the SPA exhausting their retirement savings during
                        their lifetime.

               (d)      individuals over the SPA who own their own home having most of
                        their property making them ‘asset rich but cash poor’.
                                                                                              [8]

     (viii) Suggest, with reasons, potential changes to the employer’s retirement pension
