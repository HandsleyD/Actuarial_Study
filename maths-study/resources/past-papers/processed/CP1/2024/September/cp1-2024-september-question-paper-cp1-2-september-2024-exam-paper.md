1    A large proprietary insurance company sells a wide range of life and general
     insurance products. The company started as a small specialist life insurance company
     but has since grown significantly. Growth has been partly due to its own successes
     and partly due to acquiring other insurance companies.

     The company is looking for new ways to expand, including launching a new
     insurance product. The new product must be innovative, both in terms of product
     design and customer experience, to gain a significant competitive advantage. An
     initial investigation carried out by the company has indicated that the new product
     should be launched and operated through a new specialist subsidiary with a different
     trading name that the company would set up.

     The company is now in discussions with the local insurance regulator to launch the
     new specialist subsidiary.

     There have been some complaints made by customers. The complaints refer to the
     poor quality of the data that the company holds and its data security. A recent article
     in the press has highlighted these complaints made by the customers.

     The regulator has written to the company asking for the company’s response to the
     complaints about the company’s poor data quality and security.

     The regulator has indicated that it is considering not authorising the new insurance
     product or the establishment of the new specialist subsidiary until it is satisfied with
     the response on the poor data quality and security issue.

     As part of an initial investigation, the company has reviewed the policy type held by
     the customers that have made the complaints, a single unit life product called ‘SUL’.
     By comparing the customer’s annual statements from this year and last year, the
     company has identified a significant error in the customer’s fund value.

     (i)     Discuss why the company wishes to expand.                                     [10]

     (ii)    Suggest possible actions the company could take to gain a significant
             competitive advantage in offering the new insurance product.                  [10]

     (iii)   Discuss the possible advantages of using a new subsidiary to launch the new
             product.                                                                   [7]

     (iv)    Discuss the factors the regulator should consider before deciding to take action
             against the insurance company.                                                [6]

     The company is investigating the possible causes of the errors in the fund value
     shown in the customer’s annual statements.

     (v)     Set out the steps the insurer could take when carrying out their investigation.
                                                                                           [6]

     (vi)    Describe various checks that the company could perform to determine if the
             latest unit reserves on its SUL policies are correct.                    [13]

2    A large company that sponsors a large benefit scheme for its employees, has recently
     purchased a number of companies, which each had small employee benefit schemes.
     These schemes are currently managed separately.

     The various scheme managers are undertaking several significant projects.

           Project A: Updating an existing benefits calculation model to use an automatic
            market data feed rather than rely on manual inputs of the data.
           Project B: Improving the quality of membership data held on its systems by
            writing to members to obtain more information about them and their dependants.
           Project C: Transferring a proportion of one of the scheme’s liabilities to an
            insurance company. In return for a single premium, the insurance company will
            take on the liability for making future benefit payments to the transferring
            members. The premium will be paid based on initial membership data, with both
            parties having 12 months to agree final data. The final premium payable will be
            based on finalised data and the insurance company will determine whether the
            scheme needs to pay additional funds or not.
           Project D: Merging all of the employee benefit schemes into one scheme.
           Project E: Creating a member website where all information about the schemes
            and the benefits for each member can immediately be accessed.

     (i)       Outline the criteria that the scheme managers should use to prioritise the five
               projects set out above.                                                       [3]

     (ii)      Discuss the rationale for Project A.                                          [5]

     (iii)     Outline how the scheme can test that the benefits calculation model still
               works after Project A has been implemented.                                   [5]

     (iv)      Outline possible issues with gathering data about the members and their
               dependants in Project B.                                                      [5]

     (v)       Describe how the scheme could allow for the results from Project B when
               setting its demographic assumptions.                                    [5]

     (vi)      Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the transfer in Project C.        [5]

     (vii)     Discuss the possible risks to the insurance company of agreeing the
               transaction in Project C based on initial membership data.                    [4]

     (viii) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages to the company of merging the
            schemes in Project D.                                                            [5]
