4     A company sponsors a defined benefit pension scheme (the Scheme), which is closed
      to new members. The Scheme is very mature with the majority of members being
      pensioners.

      The company is considering transferring the Scheme to a non-insurance consolidator,
      which is a commercial organisation that accepts responsibility for pension schemes.
      This would work as follows:

            The assets and liabilities of the Scheme would be transferred to the consolidator.
            The consolidator may require a premium to be paid in addition to the Scheme’s
             assets to cover any shortfall.
            The consolidator would become responsible for funding, and paying, the
             members’ benefits and the link to the company and the Scheme’s trustees would
             be broken.
            Multiple pension schemes can be transferred to the consolidator.

      The company has asked its actuarial adviser to consider if transferring the Scheme to
      a consolidator would be appropriate.

      (i)       Outline the key financial and demographic assumptions the actuarial adviser
                would need to estimate the cost of transferring the Scheme to a consolidator.
                                                                                            [4]

      The actuarial adviser has estimated the cost of the following two options:

            Option A: Transferring the Scheme to a non-insurance consolidator.
            Option B: A buy-out, with annuities for all members purchased from an insurance
             company.

                                   Option A                      Option B
                        Non-insurance consolidator option Insurance buy-out option
                                  (£ millions)                  (£ millions)
          Assets                      540                           540
          Liabilities                 550                           580
          Cost                         10                            40

      The cost, shown in the final row in the table indicates the additional contribution
      required in excess of the Scheme’s assets, to fund each option.

      (ii)      Suggest possible reasons why the estimated costs of Option A and Option B
                are different to each other.                                              [5]

5     A large occupational defined benefit pension scheme (the Scheme) was closed in
      2020 to both new members and future accrual of benefits. The sponsoring employer
      established a defined contribution scheme for provision of pension benefits after this
      date.

      The latest valuation of the Scheme has shown an improvement to the Scheme’s
      funding position. The results of the valuation, determined using the projected unit
      method of funding, show that the Scheme is currently 99% funded on the Scheme’s
      funding basis and 85% funded on a buy-out basis.

      The Scheme’s assets are invested 80% in government bonds and 20% in equities.

      (i)       Explain why the Scheme’s funding level on a buy-out basis is lower than on
                the Scheme’s funding basis.                                               [4]

      The sponsoring employer has been considering measures to reduce its costs. A
      member of the finance team has heard that the following measures could reduce the
      employer’s pension contributions:

            change the Scheme’s funding method to the basic form of the current unit method
            increase the proportion of the Scheme’s assets invested in equities to 30%.

      (ii)      Discuss the impact that these measures may have on the employer’s
                contributions into the Scheme.                                             [10]

      (iii)     Explain why these measures may not be implemented in practice.               [2]

      The sponsoring employer has written to the Scheme’s trustees to say that it is
      experiencing trading difficulties.

      (iv)      Outline the advantages and disadvantages of the different ways that the
                trustees could assess the employer’s covenant.                               [7]

      (v)       Describe possible actions that the Scheme’s trustees could take to reduce the
                employer’s contributions into the Scheme, without compromising the security
                of members’ benefits.                                                        [4]
