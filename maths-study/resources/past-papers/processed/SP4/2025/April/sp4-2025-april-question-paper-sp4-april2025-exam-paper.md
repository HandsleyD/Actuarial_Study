1    Describe the main ways defined benefit pension schemes can be financed.                  [4]


2    A defined benefit pension scheme provides members with an annual pension on
     retirement at age 67. Once the pension starts to be paid it will increase each year in
     line with price inflation capped at 5% p.a. If the member dies while in retirement,
     their spouse will receive 50% of the member’s pension at the date of the member’s
     death.

     At retirement, members can choose to take one or both of the following options:

           Exchange up to 25% of their own pension for an immediate cash lump sum, on
            terms of £15 for every £1 of annual pension given up.
           Exchange all future pension increases for a flat non-increasing pension, which is
            25% higher at retirement than the increasing pension.

     Discuss the issues that members should consider when deciding whether to take one
     or both of the options.                                                         [9]


3    A manufacturing company sponsors a defined benefit pension scheme. Due to the
     automation of one of the company’s product lines, the company intends to restructure
     its workforce and reduce the number of people it employs.

     The company is proposing to offer a cash incentive of £5,000 to any employee who
     leaves employment and takes early retirement from the pension scheme. The incentive
     will be added to each employee’s final salary payment from the company.

     (i)       Identify, with reasons, the parties that the company will need to involve for
               this proposal to succeed.                                                     [2]

     (ii)      Discuss the considerations for the scheme managers of the likely impacts of
               the proposal on the pension scheme.                                         [9]

     (iii)     Discuss the risks to the company that may arise were the proposal to proceed.

4    A large defined benefit pension scheme (the Scheme) is due to carry out its next
     funding valuation.

     Funding valuations occur every 3 years. The mortality assumption in the Scheme’s
     funding valuation has remained unchanged for the past four funding valuations.

     (i)      Explain why it may not be appropriate to continue to use the same mortality
              assumption for the Scheme’s next funding valuation.                         [4]

     The Scheme’s trustees have asked the Scheme’s actuary to update the mortality
     assumption in the Scheme’s next funding valuation.

     (ii)     Outline how the Scheme’s actuary may derive an updated mortality
              assumption.                                                                 [5]

     (iii)    Describe how the Scheme’s trustees could monitor the appropriateness of the
              mortality assumption at future funding valuations.                        [4]

     Following the review of the mortality assumptions for the funding valuation, the
     sponsoring employer is considering how this will impact the mortality assumptions
     used in their accounting disclosures.

     (iv)     Discuss why the mortality assumption used in the accounting disclosures may
              differ to that used in the funding valuation.                             [3]

     (v)      Give other possible examples of where mortality assumptions may be used in
              relation to the Scheme.                                                  [2]
